# Changelog

Notable changes to Ollin, newest first. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/). Version numbers follow semantic versioning at major zero: a breaking change or a new feature bumps the minor, and a fix bumps the patch. Each release names its breaking renames here, so pin `.upToNextMinor` and read the entry before you move up.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Mohamed Melehi joins the recreations.** `Examples/Recreations/MohamedMelehi` is new, after the hard-edge wave paintings of the 1970s, the folder's first artist from Africa. `Waves` sends one ribbon of flat bands across the panel: it enters straight and breaks into the wave part of the way over, every band edge is one curve moved over by the band's width so neighbors share their edge point for point, the palette hands its colors out by declared shares in its own order, and the wave travels because the phase is the clock, one wavelength per cycle, which is the export loop. `Flamme` stands the wave up: a row of pink and navy bands rises from the bottom edge, each cut flat at its own height, the tops stepping up to a peak and down again so the row reads as one flame, every top breathing within the steps to its neighbors, a straight ribbon of yellow, orange and blue crossing behind. Both export every band as one closed outline. Read off the SVG: every band's two sides differ by one constant vector of exactly one band width across the run, neighbors' shared edges are written identically, each edge fits the wave on the clock's phase to the file's precision, and the fills count out the shares; read off the PNG, every pixel along a shared edge is a blend of its two fills and never the ground.

- **The handoffs run under Thread Sanitizer.** `Scripts/test.sh tsan` builds the package a second time with the sanitizer, under `.build/tsan`, and runs every target whose tests run without the GPU: the wires, the phone's codecs and readers, the audio engine, the mutation harness, and the feeds, where a producer on a thread of its own hands a sketch what it read through a lock, a convention no tool had checked before. Any report fails the run and the reports print after it; one class of report, an ordering that runs through a system framework the sanitizer cannot see into, is excused per entry in `Scripts/tsan-suppressions.txt`; `Scripts/preflight.sh --milestone` runs it. `Tests/README.md` says what is in, what stays out and why, and what a run costs. The committed tree reports nothing. [The test suite](Tests/README.md)

- **A second visit to Gego.** `Examples/Recreations/Gego` gains two sketches. `Tejedura` is after the *Tejeduras* (1988 to 1991), the woven sheets of her last years: a print of one of her nets is pulled twice, one impression kept whole and the other cut into warp strips of uneven width and woven with strips of cigarette-packet foil, weft over warp, the plain rhythm broken where a strip floats over two or three crossings, and every cell of the weave showing whichever strip is on top there, so the print goes on reading through the foil. `Chorro` is after the *Chorros* (1970 to 1971): falls of rods hooked end to end, each rod a rigid body on a ball joint, hung from a plate the air moves, tied into rings and triangles on the way down, and let down to the floor, where whatever is left over lies out. Drag a rod to take hold of it.

- **Kuramoto oscillators couple over a lattice or a graph, and say where they have locked.** `Kuramoto(columns:rows:layout:wraps:)` lays a crowd on a square (`.square(.vonNeumann)` or `.square(.moore)`) or hex (`.hex`) lattice, each oscillator pulled by the sites within `range` rings of its own, with `wraps` for a torus; the hex lattice shares `HexGrid`'s addressing, so the cell `hexGrid(columns:rows:)[i]` draws is site `i`. `Kuramoto(neighbors:)` couples over any list of neighbor sites, and the settable `neighbors` hands any crowd a graph. `localCoherence` is the order parameter over each oscillator's own neighborhood, the map of where the crowd has locked. A crowd can hold several `layers` of oscillators per site, each layer coupled over the same neighborhoods and a site's layers pulled together by `layerCoupling`. `rates` is every oscillator's rate at the last substep, the natural pace plus the pull, for a motion blur or a trail of whatever the crowd drives. The ring and the mean field step exactly as before. `Docs/Simulation/Oscillators.md`; the `Simulation/Kuramoto` example gains a honeycomb field (issue #25). [Oscillators](Docs/Simulation/Oscillators.md)

- **A second visit to Hassan Sharif.** `Examples/Recreations/HassanSharif` gains two sketches. `OctoberLines` is after *10th to 13th October No. 1 & No. 2* (1984), the semi-system whose rule is all on its second sheet: a table of two-digit numbers, a wavy line that cuts it, the numbers on the line's left summed digit by digit, the repeats dropped, and the sums ruled as bands of lines, straight and wavy in turn, as many lines as the sum; here chance draws the cut, the sheet writes the list and the red box, and the pen rules the bands on the sheet and on the wall beside it. `BodyAndSquares` is after the 1983 performance of that name: a grid of twenty-five squares the size of a body, five squares picked by chance for the head, hands and feet, the body relaxed onto them along fixed bone lengths, a pick it cannot reach struck through and picked again, and every square the body lies across filled in on the record beside a print of the position.

- **Nasreen Mohamedi joins the recreations.** `Examples/Recreations/NasreenMohamedi` is new, after the untitled ink and graphite drawings of the 1970s and of about 1980, the folder's first artist from South Asia. `Registers` rules a square sheet edge to edge: heavy lines cut the field into registers, each register holds a run of fine lines whose intervals are a geometric progression, so the lines gather against one heavy line and open toward the other, and a line's weight follows its interval; the upper half is doubled on the right, a few verticals rise from the bottom edge, pencil guides stand under everything, and over one cycle each register's ratio drifts so the lines tighten and relax while the heavy lines hold still. `Diagonals` floats a figure on a wide sheet: one to four chevrons along a slant, each one stroke whose width is full at the corner and thins to the tip of each arm through a stroke profile, with a wake of hairlines parallel to the long arm laid along the short one at growing intervals, and a fan of hairlines from stations on the short arm toward one point well off the sheet; over the cycle the figure slides a little along its axis, the fans sweep and the wakes breathe. Both export for a pen: every hairline as a line, and a chevron as the outline of the region its stroke covers, so the thinning survives on paper.

- **A second visit to Manuel Felguérez.** `Examples/Recreations/ManuelFelguerez` gains two sketches, after *El espacio múltiple* (1973) and the lacquered reliefs that followed the aesthetic machine. `EspacioMultiple` builds one form-idea from a square with rounded corners, a rectangle, a disk, a half disk and a right triangle, and carries it through its three states: the flat painting in a silver or a gold scheme, the relief the planes rise into under a light that rakes across the wall, and the standing piece the relief comes apart into, where the sheet becomes the floor, the band a shelf through the square and the displacement depth; a new combination of colors, displacement, rows and heights arrives with each cycle, and the cycle is the export loop. `stage` holds one state still, `unlit` shows that the relief seen straight on is the painting again, and `--export-svg` gives the plan. `RelieveLacado` composes and weighs a design exactly as `MaquinaEstetica` does, so the same seed is the same design in both, and raises it in lacquered layers, the largest element lowest and the pen's outline as a plate of aluminum, under a key light that circles slowly; `view` walks round it, sees it straight on, or shows the plan.

### Fixed

- **The API diff reads a conformance to the standard library.** A type that became a `Collection` and dropped its own `count`, `first` and `isEmpty` read as three removals and a rename nobody made (`count` is `endIndex`), a major and three shim violations from 1.0 on, though every call site still compiled. `Scripts/api-diff.sh` now reads such a member as moved onto the protocol that still supplies it, from a table of what `Sequence`, the `Collection` family, `Equatable`, `Hashable`, `Comparable`, `Identifiable`, `CustomStringConvertible` and the string literals give a conforming type, and its self-test compiles a call against every line of that table. The report's rename count no longer counts a type that only gained conformances. [The API listings](API/README.md)

- **A still counts its frame at the rate `--fps` names.** `--export`, `--export-exr`, `--export-plates` and the vector exports read `--frame` and never `--fps`, so `--frame 30 --fps 10` was drawn at half a second rather than three, and a still never matched the sequence it was meant to sit in at any rate but 60. Every one-frame export now reads both flags the way the sequence does. [Export](Docs/Output/Export.md)

- **An extension's `afterFrame` fires in an export.** The headless drives never called it, so `FrameInfo.profile` could be read from a window and never from the run that mattered. It now fires once per rendered frame, with the export clock's rate and step and the frame's own unsmoothed times, the renderer timing its encode, GPU run and wait on the headless path too. See [Profiling](Docs/Tools/Profiling.md).

- **A `--param` value is there when `setup()` runs.** It landed only after `setup()`, so a count built there, or an extension registered behind a switch, read the declared default. It is now applied before `setup()` and again after it, so `setup()` reads the run's value and a `setup()` that assigns the parameter itself still does not undo it. The canvas follows: the value is on the sketch before `canvasSize` is read, so a `canvasSize` that picks its size by a parameter exports and opens at the run's size, where it took the declared default. [Parameters](Docs/Helpers/Parameters.md)

- **A stranger's packet no longer ends the sketch.** Every decoder that reads a network or a cable now runs under a mutation harness in the test suite (truncations, bit flips, splices, length fields pushed to their extremes, numbers written as text), and what it found is fixed. A Firmata board sending a one-byte command, a program change, took the parser past its data. An OSC float or double too large for an `Int`, an MQTT payload reading `1e300`, a room value carrying the same number, and a stepper or menu index sent over OSCQuery or from the remote page each trapped on the narrowing, and now read as `nil` or are ignored like a value of the wrong kind. A WebSocket frame announcing a length one short of what an `Int` holds overflowed the check that read it, and a frame past 1 MiB now drops the connection instead. A Link peer announcing a beat or a time past two to the fiftieth overflowed the tempo math on joining, and the entry is now skipped. A depth, matte, saliency or flow map from the phone whose width times height overflows is refused at the wire. A table cell reading `1e300` trapped the same way through `int`. [The test suite](Tests/README.md)

- **The aesthetic machine weighed a quarter disk off center, and drew its ring as a cut disk.** `MaquinaEstetica` placed a wedge's weight at 0.3 of its radius from the corner rather than 4 / 3π, so a design with a wedge was kept or refused against the wrong center, and it drew its quarter ring through `drawPolygon`, which fans a concave outline, so the ring's inner arc came out as a straight chord. Both are fixed, and the sheets a seed produces change wherever a wedge or a ring appears.

- **A line spray whose scene changes size no longer remakes its particles every frame.** Under `.byLength` sampling, lines that stretch or surfaces that breathe move the pass's total by a few points each frame, and the buffer all the points land in was sized to that total exactly, so it was thrown away and made again every frame: at six million points, 576 MB of new memory a frame, enough to stall the desktop under a live window. The particles are now kept and only grown, with room for the total to drift. A scene that only turns never saw it, since its lengths hold. See [Depth of field from light](Docs/Drawing/DepthOfField.md).

- **The lens flare's ghosts were in the wrong places, at the wrong sizes, since it shipped.** The matrices that place a ghost went wrong on the leg between its two reflections: they passed *through* the inner reflecting surface before reflecting off it, and they mixed two sign conventions for a ray running back toward the front. Every ghost came out misplaced, missized, and so misweighted in brightness, and nearly all of them landed on the light's own side of the frame, which is why the flare never looked like a chain through the middle. The angle the coating was read at also had the wrong sign on a surface's lean. The only check the matrices had was the straight-through path, which has no reflection in it. They are now checked against real rays followed through the same glass, to parts in a million, for every ghost of both bundled lenses. Expect a flare that looks different: ghosts on both sides of the frame's center, in a sane spread of brightness. The ghost polygons also line up with the star's arms now, where they used to sit turned by an angle that changed with `starSize`. [Lens flare](Docs/3D/LensFlare.md)

### Changed

- **One shape per kind of failure, first half.** A failure a call can see for itself throws, and one that happens later is readable afterwards; several kinds took two shapes, and each is one now. `VideoPlayer(url:)` throws `VideoError.fileNotFound` for a file that is not there, as the `path:` and `resource:` doors already did, and a file that opens but is not a movie says so in the new `VideoPlayer.unavailableReason` instead of never producing a frame. `Record3DRecording.pose(at:)` throws `frameOutOfRange` outside the recording, as `frame(at:)` does, and is `nil` only for a frame with no recorded pose. `ImpulseResponse.load(_:)`, `.resource(_:withExtension:in:)` and `init(contentsOf:)`, `GrainSource(named:withExtension:in:)` and `(contentsOf:)`, and `SampledInstrument(sfz:in:)` and `(contentsOf:)` throw `AudioError` (`resourceNotFound`, `couldNotDecode`, and the new `empty`) where they returned `nil` beside a printed note. An audio engine that will not start is written to a new `unavailableReason` on `Synth`, `Tone` and `AudioPlayer`, cleared by the next start that succeeds, where `Synth.start()` printed and `Tone.play()` and `AudioPlayer.play()` swallowed it; `AudioInput.start()` still throws for what it can see and now writes `unavailableReason` for a refused, denied or restricted microphone, which used to leave it silent. `PhysicsSnapshot(data:)` and `(resource:withExtension:in:)` throw `PhysicsSnapshot.Failure` (`unreadable`, and the new `resourceNotFound`, each printing as its sentence) where they returned `nil`; `World3D.restore(_:)` throws `unreadable` where it printed, and `World3D.load(contentsOf:)` throws where it returned `false`. `World3D.addVehicle`, `addRagdoll(from:)`, `addSoftBody(from:)`, `addRope(through:)` and `addTensegrity(_:)` throw the new `PhysicsError.unbuildable`, whose text says what was needed, where they returned `nil` and printed it. A sketch that stored one of these in an optional writes `try?` at the call and keeps its shape. [Video](Docs/Video/Video.md), [Audio](Docs/Helpers/Audio.md), [Synthesis](Docs/Helpers/Synthesis.md), [3D physics](Docs/Simulation/Physics3D.md), [Record3D](Docs/3D/Record3D.md)

- **One shape per failure, second half: the failures that had no shape, and the errors nothing could reach.** `SerialPort` and `FirmataBoard` gain `lastError`, the sentence `open()` is waiting on (`no device at /dev/cu.usbmodem1101`, a port another program holds, `the device went away`), cleared the moment the port opens, and a device that went away keeps that sentence while the retries only confirm the absence, a retry that fails some other way replacing it; a wrong path, a missing device and a device that went away used to be one silent state behind `isOpen`. `SyphonClient` gains `unavailableReason` for a source that was not there when it looked, no Metal device, a refused connection, or a source that has since gone away. `LinkClock` gains `interfaceCount` and `unavailableReason`, so a running clock that could open no network interface says so instead of reading exactly like one alone on the network. `OSCQueryServer` and `RemoteInspector` gain `isRunning`, `unavailableReason` and a `start()` that serves again after `stop()` or tries the port again after a failed start; a port another program holds used to be a printed line and a listener that could never be started again, and `RemoteInspector.stop()` no longer leaves `boundPort` set. `VirtualCameraError` is internal, since no public call ever produced it; `VirtualCameraServer.unavailableReason` carried its sentences already. The five `Error` enums nested in `ModelTracker`, `PointSegmenter`, `ConceptTracker`, `DepthTracker` and `DepthClip` are one `VisionError` with `unavailable` and the new `failed`; a `catch` naming one of the old types names `VisionError` now. `PersonSegmenter.detect(in:quality:)` and `SaliencyTracker.detect(in:mode:)` return their value rather than an Optional and throw `VisionError.failed` when a matte or heat map cannot be converted, where a conversion failure used to come back as `nil`; `SubjectSegmenter.detect(in:)` is `nil` only when no subject is found. The depth tracker no longer drops its own model's failure on the floor: a model that answers no depth map lands in `unavailableReason`. [Serial](Docs/Integration/Serial.md), [Syphon](Docs/Integration/Syphon.md), [Link](Docs/Integration/Link.md), [OSCQuery](Docs/Integration/OSCQuery.md), [Remote](Docs/Integration/Remote.md), [Vision](Docs/Vision/Vision.md)

- **A number that does not fit reads as `nil`.** `OSCArgument.int`, `MQTTMessage.int` and `int(named:)`, `RoomMessage.int`, and a table's `int(_:)` return `nil` for a number that is not finite or is too large for an `Int`, where they trapped before; a parameter restored from a stored `.number` of that size keeps its value. [OSC](Docs/Integration/OSC.md), [MQTT](Docs/Integration/MQTT.md), [Room](Docs/Integration/Room.md)

- **The lens flare is followed ray by ray.** A ghost used to be a flat, evenly lit copy of the iris in one color, worked out to first order. Each ghost is now a grid of real rays carried through the lens's actual spherical surfaces, so it comes out the way glass makes it: its sides stretched and leaning, more the further the light sits from the middle; cut by the round barrel wherever a ray strays past the rim of an element; brighter along a rim or in a core where neighboring rays land together, which is a caustic; and lit by a coating read at the angle each ray really met it at. A ghost bright enough for it to show is followed in seven wavelengths, each through the glass that color sees (`LensInterface.abbeNumber`, with the numbers the patents print on both bundled lenses), so its colors part smoothly at the rim. From a small source the light rings just inside each edge. `LensFlare.sourceSize` now sets how soft every ghost is, and a ghost near focus seen from a wide source becomes a small soft picture of it, round for a round source whatever the iris's shape. The star gains `LensFlare.wear`, the wear on the opening: uneven and bowed blades split and fray the arms, and specks and scratches fill the space between them with fine needles. `Lens.doubleGauss` is a second bundled lens, a 1950s six-element double Gauss whose twenty ghosts make the chain a flare is known for. Three extras are off until asked for: `LensFlare.streak` (with `streakLength`, `streakAngle`, `streakTint`) is the long thin line cylindrical glass fans a light into, the one an anamorphic lens or a streak filter throws; `LensFlare.dirt` is grime on the front element, each speck a soft picture of the iris that glows where it sits near the light; and `LensFlare.halo` (with `haloSize`) is the rainbow ring flare artwork draws around a light, which is a look and not optics, and says so. `Lens.anamorphic(squeeze:)` puts an anamorphic front group on a lens: the ghosts, the star, and the halo stretch sideways by the squeeze, while a ghost's edge stays as soft across as up and a ghost near focus stays a round picture of a round source; the streak stays a line and the dirt on the front keeps its shape. **What moves in an existing sketch:** `lensFlare()` with no lens named now uses `Lens.standard`, the double Gauss multicoated at f/8, where it used the Heliar wide open (name `.heliar` to keep it; with its ghosts in their true places it veils the frame more than it chains across it); the level is set once per lens instead of per frame off the hottest ghost; closing the iris brightens the ghosts it shrinks, by as much as it shrinks them, and dims the star's arms as it lengthens them; `multicoated()` hands its wavelengths out so that neighboring surfaces land far apart, which changes which color each ghost comes out; and `sourceSize` defaults to `0.015` (was `0.035`). It costs from about one millisecond a frame to about five at 1080 square on an M2, by how much of the frame the lens's ghosts fill. [Lens flare](Docs/3D/LensFlare.md)

## [0.10.0] - 2026-09-19

### Added

- **Coming from openFrameworks or OPENRNDR.** The Guide's new Appendix E covers the big moves from either one, each as a pair of code followed by what's different. From openFrameworks: the app as one class with no `update()`, fill and stroke as two inks, `withState` for both stacks, an `ofFbo` as a layer, `ofShader` as one `shade` function in Metal, the camera as a call, the C++ loop-by-reference habit, the addons as satellite modules, projectGenerator as `ollin new`, and rebuilding as live reload. From OPENRNDR: Kotlin to Swift, the program as a sketch, helpers that extend `Sketch` rather than the drawer, the value types, `rotate` taking radians where OPENRNDR takes degrees, the compositor, shade styles, and the orx modules. Appendix C stays the full call-by-call map for p5.js and Processing. [Appendix E](Guide/E-ComingFromOpenFrameworksAndOPENRNDR.md)

- **A black hole, drawn by following light past it.** `Examples/Shaders/BlackHole` traces every pixel's ray backward past a Schwarzschild horizon with the orbit equation for light, so the shadow, the far side of the disk standing over the hole and under it, stars smeared into arcs, and a star behind the hole seen as a ring all fall out of the physics rather than being painted. The thin disk shines as a black body at the temperature Page and Thorne's flux gives each radius, shifted by the climb out of the well and by the gas's own motion: blue-white on the side turning toward the camera, red on the side turning away, as Luminet drew it in 1979. Drag to move round the hole, scroll to come closer; a grid sky shows the folding on lines of latitude and longitude. The physics is its own file, `physics.metal`, which the picture includes, and `BlackHoleLensTests` compiles that file alone and checks it against closed forms: the swept angle against the exact orbit integral, the far-field bend against 4GM/(c²b), the shadow's edge against Synge's angle, the ring against the exact orbit and, far off, the weak-field Einstein ring, and the color of heat against the CIE tables. It is the heaviest user shader in the examples and plays on the web page too.

- **An outline answers questions about itself.** A `Contour` can say which way it runs at a fraction along it (`tangent(at:)`, `normal(at:)`), where it passes nearest a point and how far along that is (`nearestPoint(to:)`, `fraction(of:)`, `distance(to:)`), and hand back the stretch between two fractions (`piece(from:to:)`). It finds where it meets another outline or crosses itself (`crossings(with:)`, `crossings()`), each crossing carrying its point and how far along both lines it sits. It thins a dense trace to the points it needs (`simplified(tolerance:)`), rounds or cuts its corners (`rounded(_:)`, `chamfered(_:)`, also on `Shape`), and walks itself backward as a `Contour` (`reversed()`). `Examples/Shapes/OverUnder` weaves a tangled loop over and under through its own crossings. [Geometry](Docs/Drawing/Geometry.md)

- **A homage after Vladimir Bonačić.** `Examples/Recreations/VladimirBonacic` rebuilds two of the dynamic objects he made in Zagreb between 1969 and 1971, lamps driven by the arithmetic of Galois fields. `DynamicObject` is the 32 by 32 square of lamps of *GF.E 32-S*, built from the arithmetic he published in 1974: each lamp belongs to the residue class `row ^ column`, the pattern is an element of the field of 2^32 elements modulo x^32 + x^22 + x^2 + x + 1 whose bit i lights class i, and every tick multiplies it by x, so the walk passes all 4,294,967,295 patterns before one repeats. The back of the object is drawn too, 32 indicators and 32 buttons that read the element in binary or set it. `NamaFrieze` is *DIN. PR 18*, the frieze of eighteen lamp panels that ran 36 meters across a department store front on Kvaternik Square, stepping through all 262,143 states of an 18-bit field with a second, shorter register gating the rhythm. Set `sendsToWall` and the panels go out over sACN as eighteen dimmer channels, so the same universe lights a real wall.

- **The types that are a list of things say so.** A `Contour` is its points, a `Shape` is its contours, a `Grid`, `HexGrid`, or `TriangleGrid` is its cells, and a `Polyomino` is its squares, so all of them now read as collections: `for point in contour`, `grid.count`, `piece.contains(cell)`, `shape.first`, `map`, `filter`, `reversed()`, `randomElement(using:)`, and the rest of the standard library, without reaching for `points` / `contours` / `cells`. A grid works each cell out as it is asked for, so looping one builds no array at all, and `cells` is still there for when an array is what you want. `Contour` also gained a geometric `contains(_ point:)`, the same question `Shape.contains(_:)` answers; ask `contour.points.contains(p)` for the vertex-membership one. [Geometry](Docs/Drawing/Geometry.md)

- **Timecode compares.** `Timecode` is `Comparable`, so a cue test is `<` and a cue window is a range: `if (cueIn..<cueOut).contains(now)`. The order runs on the moment a timecode names rather than on its frame number, so two counted at different rates still answer honestly about which came first. [MIDI](Docs/Integration/MIDI.md)

- **One frame, several lighting rigs.** Lamps are drawing state now, so the lights in force when a solid is drawn are the lights that shade it. `withLights([...]) { }` lights a block from its own fixtures and nothing else, `withLights(.noir) { }` does it from a preset, and `withoutLights { }` draws a body flat in its fill color while the scene around it stays shaded, which is how you get a hologram, a flat overlay, or a bulb that reads as a source rather than as one more shaded ball. `addLight(_:)` adds one `Light` value to the frame's own rig. Lamps set the ordinary way behave exactly as before: the frame's rig still reaches every solid wherever in `draw()` you set it, so no existing sketch changes. The frame's rig keeps the shadow casters, the environment, and the atmosphere; a scoped set lights what it draws and throws nothing. A frame holds its own set plus fifteen. [3D](Docs/3D/3D.md)

- **The depth-of-field aperture has a shape.** `Bokeh.aperture` decides what a point too far from focus turns into: `.round` as before, `.blades(6)` for the hexagonal sparkle a real iris draws, `.blades(count:rotation:)` to turn it, or `.picture(_:)` for a mask read across the hole. That last one takes any `Image`, so an iris a sketch drew into a render target and one it loaded are the same path, and Ollin bundles no masks. An aperture changes where light lands, not how much of it there is, and `.round` draws exactly what it drew before. [Depth of field](Docs/Drawing/DepthOfField.md)

- **Surfaces in the depth-of-field spray.** `SprayQuad` puts a face in a `LineSpray` scene beside its wires: the parallelogram two edges span from a corner, sampled over its area, set through `setQuads(_:)`. Lines and quads scatter into one accumulator and print as one picture. A quad's `light` is the whole quad's, not a brightness per unit of area, which is the rule a line already kept. `LineSpray.picture` is an image the quads read through each quad's own `pictureBounds`, so a photograph or a sheet of glyphs becomes an object made of light for the lens to throw out of focus. Purely additive: nothing in the line path changed. [Depth of field](Docs/Drawing/DepthOfField.md)

- **A homage after Gego.** `Examples/Recreations/Gego` takes up the two bodies of work she is best known for. `Reticularea` ties a net of irregular triangles, hangs it from the ceiling at eight points and lets gravity have it: the triangles the sketch draws are the triangles the simulation holds, there is more net than the ties are spread over so it falls into folds, and the whole thing is drawn as 2D lines through `project`, thinner and paler as they go away from you. `DrawingWithoutPaper` hangs a bent wire lattice a few centimeters off a wall and crosses a lamp in front of it, and the shadow it throws is the drawing. Drag a wire in either one.

- **MIDI files in and out.** `MIDIFile` reads a Standard MIDI File into notes a sketch can play or draw, and writes one back out, so a piece written elsewhere runs in a sketch and a phrase a sketch worked out opens in a sequencer. Formats 0, 1, and 2, the tempo map read from either side with `beats(at:)` and `seconds(at:)`, plus control changes, pitch bends, programs, names, and markers. A `Synth` will also write down what it is asked to play: `startRecording(tempo:name:)` and `stopRecording()` hand back a take. It lives in `OllinAudio`, since a file arrives as notes rather than as messages. [MIDI files](Docs/Helpers/MIDIFiles.md)

### Changed

- **Deriving one easing curve from another.** `Easing.reversed()` runs a curve from its other end, so an ease-in becomes its ease-out, and `mirrored()` puts the curve on the first half of a trip and its reverse on the second. Where the catalog already holds the result the helper hands back the built-in, so it still reads by name on a menu. `.easeInQuad.reversed() == .easeOutQuad`, and mirroring an ease-out gives the out-in curve the catalog lacks.

It sits in the chain like the built-in rooms, and turning `mix` never cuts a tail that is sounding. An `ImpulseResponse` comes from `.load`, `.resource`, `.decay(seconds:damping:)`, or a rule; `synth.reverb` still reaches it, and `Effect.Kind` gains `.convolution`.

The `Timecode` value does the drop-frame arithmetic and both wire forms, and `MIDIOutput.send(timecode:)` and `send(sysEx:)` cover the other direction. [MIDI](Docs/Integration/MIDI.md#timecode-timecodeclock)
- **Sharp features from a field: dual contouring.** `isosurface(at:in:resolution:method: .dualContouring)` meshes a field with one vertex per cube placed where the field's own normals meet, so a block's corners land on the corners and a bored hole keeps its rim, where marching cubes rounds every edge off to the size of a cell. [Isosurfaces](Docs/Generators/Isosurface.md#sharp)
- **A homage after Josef Albers.** `Examples/Recreations/JosefAlbers/Homage` puts the *Homage to the Square* format on the canvas, squares of ten, eight, six, and four units each set half a unit lower than the one around it. It rolls a new set of colors on every few seconds from five palette rules of its own, built in OKLCH and crossfaded in OKLab. `--export-grid` prints a wall of them.
- **Search on the website.** Every page `ollin site` writes carries a search: the button in the bar, `/`, or ⌘K opens it, and it looks through the Guide, the reference, and the examples one section at a time, a page's title counting most and its prose least. The matching is [MiniSearch](https://lucaong.github.io/minisearch/) (MIT), pinned to one release by its hash.
- **Embroidery, a frame as stitches.** `--export-embroidery leaf.dst` writes the frame as the stitches an embroidery machine sews, in the Tajima `.dst` file nearly every machine reads: strokes as a running stitch whose every corner is a penetration, fills as connected rows, and each color as its own thread in draw order. `Embroidery(width:)` takes the hoop's real millimeters. `Embroidery.stitches(_:in:)` plans your own contours, so a sketch can draw the stitches before anything sews.
- **Brushwork, the picture as paint.** `layer.filtered(.brushwork())` lays the layer on as paint patches that follow the picture's own flow: each pixel takes the average of the flattest of eight sectors of a brush around it, so detail flattens while edges stay crisp. `radius` is the brush size and `stretch` how far an edge can draw it out. `sharpness` is how decisively the flattest sector wins, and the patches read as strokes along the contours rather than `.oilPaint`'s square dabs.
- **Lighter, darker, and the complement of a color.** `ink.lighter()`, `ink.darker(by: 0.25)`, and `ink.complement` derive a color from one you hold: the first two step its perceived lightness in OKLab units while hue and chroma stay put, the third is the opposite hue at the same lightness and chroma. A gray is its own complement.
- **Gabor noise, a noise whose spectrum you design.** `generate(.gaborNoise())` scatters small Gaussian blobs each carrying a cosine wave and sums them, so the field has one `wavelength` in pixels, a band `bandwidth` wide around it, and one direction or every direction. Brushed metal, wood grain, silk, and rippled water at a turn of the parameters. `impulses` is the quality and `seed` picks the field. The same field is `gaborNoise(x, y, ...)` and the `GaborNoise` type on the CPU, and `gaborNoise(p, ...)` in a shader of your own.
- **XDoG, the picture as pen and ink.** `layer.filtered(.xdog())` draws a line wherever the layer has an edge, solid ink where it is dark, and paper everywhere else, the lines following each edge's own direction so they run continuous rather than breaking into speckle. `threshold` and `softness` are the cut and its ramp. `radius` is the line scale, `sharpening` how far edges are pushed over the tone, and `foreground` / `background` the ink and the paper.
- **Hobby's spline.** `drawCurve(points, spline: .hobby)` threads a curve through points the way a practiced hand does: every gap becomes a Bézier whose control points are solved together, so the bend flows evenly instead of flattening between far-apart points and swelling between close ones. The default spline is unchanged. `.hobby(tension:curl:)` tunes it, `Contour(curveThrough:spline:)` and `Shape(curveThrough:spline:)` take the same choice, and `HobbySpline(through:)` is the typed form.
- **The drag on the performance stage.** Under OllinLiveCoding, hold Command over the stage and the shape under the pointer is outlined through the code; drag it, pull a corner, or turn the knob, and the numbers change in the code on the stage, which the host then evaluates. The file is still written only by ⌘S. `⌘]` and `⌘[` reorder the same way.
- **Dashed strokes.** `strokeDash([12, 8])` cuts every stroke into dashes, dash then gap in the path's own units, and a `phase:` marches them along. Each dash takes the caps, a corner inside one keeps its join, and SVG, PDF, and G-code carry every dash as its own subpath, so a plotter lifts the pen at each gap. `noStrokeDash()` puts the whole line back, `.dots(spacing:)` under `strokeCap(.round)` is a dotted line, and `Contour.dashed(_:)` / `Shape.dashed(_:)` make the same cut as geometry. `drawCircle`, `drawRect`, `drawTriangle`, `drawNgon`, `drawStar` and their neighbors dash too while a dash is on.
- **An app for the phone, from the generator.** `ollin new <Name> --kind ios-app` writes a sketch as an iPhone and iPad app: the sketch, a host that owns the entry point, the property list, and the `project.yml` that `xcodegen` turns into the Xcode project. `--team <id>` sets the signing team. `--team <id>` or `OLLIN_TEAM` sets the signing team; `ProjectRequest` gains `team` and `Capability` gains `staysOnTheDesk`, the refusal a Mac-only capability gets.
- **The logo.** Ollin has a mark: the day sign's crossing bands with an eye at the center and four marks at the cardinal points, in plain geometry. `Logo/` holds it as two SVG masters, black ink on nothing, and the website wears it in the bar and as the favicon, setting the color itself.
- **A tempo, and note lengths with names.** `Tempo` is beats per minute with a `beatsPerBar`: `tempo.beats(at: time)` is what a `StepCounter` wants each frame and `tempo.seconds(of: .eighth)` what a synth's `for:` wants. `NoteLength` names the lengths on a stave, and a number stands in for either. `NoteLength` runs `.whole` down to `.thirtySecond` with `.dotted` and `.triplet` deriving the others, and `seconds(beats:)`, `seconds(bars:)`, `bars(at:)`, and `secondsPerBeat` read the rest.
- **Tensegrities.** `Tensegrity` describes a structure of struts held apart by cables, with three balanced forms ready-made: `prism(struts:)`, the six-strut `icosahedron(strutLength:)`, and a `tower(levels:)`. `World3D.addTensegrity(_:at:)` gives one weight, so it falls, lands on its own cables, and stands. `imbalance` says whether an arrangement of your own can hold, `drawTensegrity(_:)` draws it where it is, and a new `.cable` joint kind is the rope beside `.distance`'s rod.
- **The weather outside.** `Weather(at:)`, `Weather(in: "Oaxaca")` and their siblings read the current conditions over a place every fifteen minutes, as `temperature`, `humidity`, `cloudCover`, the wind, `condition`, and the day's `sunrise` and `sunset`. Nothing throws, a failure keeps the last reading, and an export reads once. `Weather(latitude:longitude:)` takes a point, `precipitation`, `snowfall`, `pressure` and `isDay` come with it, and `Place.sun(at:)` puts the sun in the sky with no network. [Weather](Docs/Helpers/Weather.md)
- **Strokes and fills as points on the web page.** `--export-web` carries a stroke as the points and the style the sketch gave, and the page expands them through the framework's own fringe expander compiled to WebAssembly. The six heaviest pattern examples, at 51 to 325 MB as vertices, cross at 386 KB to 8.7 MB.
- **Parquet deformations.** `parquetDeformation(columns:rows:from:to:sweep:)` lays a tiling whose tile changes shape as you read across it, a square at one edge becoming an interlocking key at the other, with no piece coming apart, because the geometry lives on the lattice's shared edges rather than on the tiles. An edge is a `Profile` in its own frame (`.straight`, `.tooth`, `.zigzag`, `.wave`, `.bump`, or `.custom`) and `Sweep` runs the change across, down, diagonally, or out from the center; read `tiles` for the pieces or `edges` for line work. [Parquet deformations](Docs/Drawing/ParquetDeformation.md)
- **A weight limit on the web page.** `--export-web` refuses a page past 25 MB instead of writing it, and the message says what it would have weighed, which part is heaviest, and the `--max-page-size` that writes it anyway. A dense enough drawing is refused during the recording, so the export never holds frames a page would not get. In code, `maxBytes:` on `OllinApp.web(of:)` and `exportWeb`, and a `WebWeightRefusal` carrying the numbers.
- **Parameters as controls on the web page.** `--export-web` offers a sketch's `@Param`s on the page: each is probed at other settings, and one whose every effect on the picture moves along a line becomes a slider, a stepper, a switch, a color well, or a field per part. One that changes what is drawn stays at its recorded value, and the exporter says why. `--no-controls` skips the probe, and the inline fragment reaches them through the handle (`params`, `get`, `set`, `reset`). [Parameters as controls](Docs/Output/Web.md#parameters-as-controls)
- **Fields on the web page.** `--export-web` carries a composed field (`drawSDF`) as its program, walked on the page over the framework's own arithmetic carried to GLSL, and a raymarched field (`drawSDF3D`) with its camera, its lights, `castShadows()`, and the four shading models. An environment, an area light, and a glass or coated finish still stop the export by name. A `raymarchResolution` below one is traced at the Mac's own fraction. [Fields on the page](Docs/Output/Web.md#fields-on-the-page)
- **A lens as a field of view.** `.focalLength(35)` is the vertical angle a 35 mm lens sees on a full-frame camera, so `fieldOfView:` takes the lens's own number beside `.degrees(_:)`; pass `sensorHeight:` for a smaller format.
- **Ramp stops as values, and a ramp reversed.** `Ramp(stops: [Ramp.Stop])` takes typed stops beside the tuple list, and `ramp.reversed` runs a ramp the other way, a hard edge keeping its colors in mirrored order.
- **Depth of field from light.** `LineSpray` renders an array of `SprayLine`s as depth of field the way a lens makes it: every frame scatters passes of points along the lines on the GPU, pushes each into a `Bokeh` ball that grows with its distance from the plane of focus, and adds it into a running mean. Nothing is blurred afterward, and moving the camera or the lens restarts the average. `makeLineSpray(_:sampling:passesPerFrame:bokeh:)`, `drawLineSpray(_:)`, and `developed(exposure:ground:)` to print it. [Depth of field from light](Docs/Drawing/DepthOfField.md)
- **A running mean of light.** `makeAccumulator()` / `withAccumulator(_:passes:)` / `Accumulator`: a layer that keeps the sum of every pass drawn into it in single-precision float and the number of passes beside it, and hands back the mean as `image`, so a picture built from faint samples converges instead of brightening without end. `reset()` starts it over, `sum` and `passes` are there for a shader of your own, `Filter.develop(exposure:ground:)` prints accumulated light, and `makeRenderTarget(precision:)` and `makeFeedback(precision:)` take `LayerPrecision.float32`. [Accumulation](Docs/Drawing/Accumulation.md#accumulator)
- **Particles as light.** `drawParticles(_:style: .light)` deposits each particle's `color × alpha × area` as linear light with no perceptual remap, and draws a particle at or under one pixel as a single texel, so a million one-pixel points cost a million fragments rather than twenty-five million. The default `.marks` style is unchanged. [The light particle style](Docs/Drawing/DepthOfField.md#light)
- **A kernel that sees the camera.** `Camera3D.viewMatrix`, `projectionMatrix(aspect:)`, `viewProjectionMatrix(aspect:)`, and `matrices(aspect:)` are public; `ComputeParams.append(camera:aspect:)` packs them, and the library's `ollin_project` projects a world point to canvas points inside a kernel. `cameraParams()` does it for the active camera, `ollin_project_eye` takes a camera-space point, and `ballSample(float3)` joins `discSample` in the library. [Projecting through the camera](Docs/Shaders/Compute.md#camera)
- **Settled frames in an export.** `--settle N` (and `OllinApp.exportSettle`) draws each written frame N times with the clock held and writes the last, so a picture that converges over frames settles for every frame of a still, a sequence, a video, or a GIF. The recipe records it; a take and made frames refuse it. An `Accumulator`'s mean or a `LineSpray` through a turning camera settles for every written frame. [Settled frames](Docs/Output/Export.md#settled-frames)
- **Compute binds up to ten buffers.** `compute(_:buffers:count:params:)` hands a kernel any number of `ComputeBuffer`s of any element types at indices 0…9, and `compute(_:reading:writing:)` now takes two element types, so a kernel can read a static geometry buffer and write an `OllinParticle` buffer without packing one into the other. `ComputeBindable` is public, and so is `Color.linearRGBA`, the radiance a light particle carries.
- **Fractal fields.** Three fractals join the raymarched field as leaves: `SDF3D.mandelbulb(power:iterations:radius:)`, `.mengerSponge(iterations:size:)`, and `.mandelbox(scale:iterations:size:)`. They melt, carve, mirror, and repeat like any other leaf, and `3D/Raymarching/RaymarchedFractals` turns each one's own dial.
- **The sketch on the phone.** A sketch runs on an iPhone or iPad as an app, and `ollin phone Sketch.swift` puts it there and installs it again on every save, its clock and parameters carried across, with the parameters live on the Mac. `SketchRunner.beginInstallation` is public, so an app of your own can turn the checkpoint on. [The sketch on the phone](Docs/Tools/OnThePhone.md)
- **A Web example group.** `Examples/Web/BreathingRing` draws the ring of breathing circles the site opens on as a sketch: a sixty-second loop that wraps without a seam, its ink and paper as parameters so a page can set its own theme.
- **A sketch as a web page.** `--export-web` records what a sketch draws over a duration and writes a page that plays it back in a browser, one self-contained file or, with `--inline`, a fragment for a page of your own. A sketch that declares `loopDuration` records one lap and the page wraps it without a seam. Anything that does not cross stops the export and names the call. [A sketch as a web page](Docs/Output/Web.md)
- **Layers and shaders on the web page.** `--export-web` carries the layered effects: layers drawn with `withTarget`, every `Generator`, every `Filter` and `Combine` that is one shader pass (plus `.gaussianBlur` and `.bloom` as passes of the page's own), `postProcess`, a user `Shader` with `info.time` and `info.mouse` live on the page, compiled `Visual` chains, and a `Feedback` layer or a single-field `SimField` keeping its state. The solves, the ladders, and the simulations with pipelines of their own still leave as video.
- **Pictures, atlas text, and gradients on the web page.** `--export-web` carries a picture drawn with `drawImage`, text through the glyph atlas as its quads over the font's page, and a gradient on an analytic shape from the strip the Mac bakes it into. A live texture, such as a camera or a compute texture, still leaves as video. The page draws its first frame once the browser has decoded the pictures, and its handle gains `ready`.
- **Strokes and fills on the web page.** `--export-web` carries every stroke and every fill the triangle path tessellates, as the vertices the Mac would have drawn, a fill's edge anti-aliased through the same four samples, and a blend mode on shapes, strokes, and fills. A dense line drawing makes a heavy page for now, and the exporter prints the size. That is `drawLine`, `drawPolyline`, `drawBezier`, `drawCurve`, `drawArc`, `drawPolygon`, and `drawShape`, and a `Batch` recorded from them replays under its transform.
- **The website.** `ollin site` writes the README, the Guide, the reference, and every example with its source out as a static website, the pages rendered from the same files `ollin docs` reads, with a workflow that publishes it to GitHub Pages. [The reference offline](Docs/Tools/Reference.md#in-a-browser)

### Changed

- **Preflight and CI, cheaper and less fragile.** `Scripts/preflight.sh` runs its gates cheapest first and the figure gate last, sizes its figure workers to the machine's memory as well as its cores, and compares each sample figure against its own last render rather than the committed image. On CI the build is cached between runs, and the Examples job skips its long build when nothing it compiles against changed.
- **The README's catalog moved to the reference index.** The capability table, the list of sixty-six generative techniques, and the 3D layer list now open [`Docs/README.md`](Docs/README.md#the-catalog), above the groups that list every page. The README's *What's in it* section keeps its eight paragraphs and points at the catalog, so the front door is the pitch and the reference is the map.
- **The website's front page is a page rather than the README end to end.** It shows the README's opening and four of its sections laid out for a browser window: the code beside its paragraph, two short sections side by side, and the eight paragraphs of *What's in it* as a grid of cards. The whole README is the About page, one link away.
- **Note lengths and tempos are typed.** `Note.length` and `Sonification.noteLength` are `NoteLength`s, and `Note.seconds(at:)`, `Synth.play(_:tempo:)`, and `Synth.play(_:step:tempo:)` take a `Tempo`. A literal compiles as it did; a `Double` variable becomes `Tempo(bpm)` or `NoteLength(beats:)`.
- **The reader-facing pages in a plain register.** Every reference page, the README, the roadmap, the example lists, and the concept pages are rewritten sentence by sentence: one idea per sentence, plain words, active voice, the same facts. Along the way about a dozen stale API names, several wrong counts, and five figures whose caption disagreed with the picture are corrected.
- **The site opens on the sketch.** The front page now plays `Examples/Web/BreathingRing` as the page `--export-web` records for it, in place of a hand-written copy of the ring: its ink and paper are set to the site's own colors and follow a change of scheme, and a reader who asked for less motion sees its first frame, still.
- **The live hosts compile the sketch optimized.** `OllinLive`, `OllinLiveCoding`, `OllinRun`, and the gallery compile a sketch with `-O`, so a sketch that does real CPU work every frame runs under the live window at the speed `swift run -c release` gives it. `--no-optimize` compiles it plain when you are debugging the sketch itself: asserts fire, and a crash names every frame.
- **Recording and frame sharing no longer draw the frame twice.** A live take, a Syphon feed, the virtual camera, an LED map, and any `SketchExtension` that asks for the rendered frame now receive the frame the window shows, tone-mapped once more inside the frame's own command buffer. A lit 3D scene that recorded at 19 fps records at its unarmed 46, and the frame arrives a refresh or so after `afterFrame`. That covers `startRecording()` and `--record`, and `wantsRenderedFrame` / `wantsRenderedTexture` are read once per frame.
- **A typed box for bounds.** `Box3` replaces the `(min: Vector3, max: Vector3)` tuple everywhere it appeared: `Scene.bounds`, `Metaballs.bounds`, `ShadowArt.bounds`, `PhoneSceneMesh.bounds`, `PhonePlanes.bounds`, and the `in:` box of `isosurface` and `shadowArt`. Reading `.min` and `.max` is unchanged; a tuple literal becomes `Box3(min:max:)`, and `center`, `size`, `longestSide`, `padded(by:)`, and `union(_:)` come with it.
- The `Rendering/DepthOfField` example runs on the accumulating pipeline: a million samples a pass deposited as light into a running mean that converges, with the focus, the lens, and the passes per frame as parameters, instead of a `noClear` pile that brightened forever.

### Fixed

- **A parameter of your own type can be declared.** The parameters page said a full `ParamValue` conformance works, but the initializer it needed was internal, so a type declared outside the framework could not be wrapped at all. `@Param(constraints:)` is now public, and the source write-back keeps a wrapper such as `Opacity(0.5)` around the literal it changes. There is a bare form for a type whose constraints are `Void`.
- **A shader beside a phone sketch reached the phone as a library, not as source.** `ollin phone` listed a `.metal` sibling in the resources build phase, which Xcode compiles; the framework reads a shader's source at run time, so the file was useless there. It now rides through a copy-files phase and arrives as the text it was.
- **A test parked a worker on the environment download.** The remote-HDRI cache had only a blocking download, right on the export path's main thread and wrong from a test body, where it held one of the concurrency pool's few threads. The cache now has an `async` `download(_:)` that suspends instead, the tests use it, and preflight refuses the blocking form under Tests.
- **A picture could park the whole run.** An image's texture was built through the synchronous MetalKit loader, which decodes on a dispatch worker and waits on its own semaphore; with every worker already blocked, as a busy test run manages, the main thread waited forever. Textures are now decoded on the calling thread and their mip chains blitted on a command buffer. The pixels are unchanged.
- **A translucent PNG composites at its own alpha.** ImageIO decodes a PNG with alpha straight, and the image pipeline blends premultiplied, so a translucent pixel of a loaded PNG drew too bright. The texture is now premultiplied on upload, as an image painted in memory already was.
- The Accumulation and HDR pages described a sum on a `noClear` canvas plus a tone map and left out the divide by the number of passes; both now describe the running mean and point at `Accumulator` and `Filter.develop`.
- The Compute reference said a kernel binds its own buffers at indices 0…9 while the entry points reached two of one type; the `buffers:` form now matches the claim.
- A raymarched field under an orthographic camera drew a soft halo at its silhouette and lost gaps narrower than the halo; the silhouette anti-aliasing now sizes its pixel cone for an orthographic projection.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-09-02

The first tagged release. Ollin grew in one repository from May 2026 to this tag, and that history is not itemized here. This entry says what the release contains, area by area, with a link to the reference for each. The [README](README.md#whats-in-it) carries the fuller catalog, and the [Guide](Guide/README.md) teaches it in order. Read this entry as a table of contents rather than a list of changes.

### The framework

- **Drawing.** A p5-style API over a Metal core: `setup()` and `draw()`, and bare calls like `drawCircle` and `background`. `draw()` runs at the display's refresh rate from the first line, so motion is the default. Some thirty analytic shapes, curved paths, concave and holed fills, variable-width strokes, marks that follow the hand, shape booleans and offsets, gradients, blend modes, clipping, accumulation, and retained batches. Every frame composites in linear light with HDR tone mapping and analytic anti-aliasing. [Drawing](Docs/Drawing/Drawing.md).
- **Text and color.** Bitmap, outline, and single-line plotter fonts through one `drawText`, in any script, in columns, justified, along a path, or as vector shapes. OKLab mixing, spectral paint mixing, palettes and colormaps, color vision simulation, and wide-gamut and HDR output. [Text](Docs/Drawing/Text.md), [Color](Docs/Drawing/Color.md).
- **Shapes that merge.** SDF combinators in 2D and in raymarched 3D: smooth union, subtraction, morphing, domain mirror, tile, and radial repeat, and a clay-like `sculpt { }` block. [Combinators](Docs/Drawing/Combinators.md).
- **Generative technique.** Sixty-six classic techniques as first-class helpers, from Poisson-disk scatter and L-systems to Wave Function Collapse, strange attractors, marching cubes, paper marbling, and terrain erosion. [Generators](Docs/Generators/README.md).
- **Layers, effects, and shaders.** Off-screen layers with fifty-plus GPU filters, two-layer combines, feedback, simulation fields, and a compose DSL. Measured distance fields, light with shadows in a flat sketch, the frequency domain, and summed-area averages. Your own `shade(uv, info)` fragment function, inline or in a hot-reloading `.metal` file, with errors reported at your own line numbers. Chainable `Visual`s when you would rather skip the Metal. [Effects](Docs/Drawing/Effects.md), [Shaders](Docs/Shaders/Shaders.md).
- **Motion and parameters.** Frame-rate-independent easing and springs, keyframe timelines, seeded variations, and record and replay. `@Param` properties become typed inspector controls, persist across reloads, and can follow a curve or a formula. [Sketch](Docs/Core/Sketch.md), [Automation](Docs/Core/Automation.md), [Variations](Docs/Core/Variations.md).
- **GPU compute and simulation.** Compute kernels with a million particles a frame, and a neighbor-search primitive under artificial life and SPH fluids. Ping-pong texture simulations, steering crowds at GPU scale, populations that evolve, and chaotic systems. [Compute](Docs/Shaders/Compute.md), [Simulation](Docs/Simulation/README.md).
- **Physics.** A stepped `World` with Verlet particles and springs, Box2D bodies and joints in 2D, and Jolt-backed 3D bodies, joints, characters, vehicles, ragdolls, cloth, ropes, and water. [Physics](Docs/Simulation/Physics.md), [Physics in 3D](Docs/Simulation/Physics3D.md).
- **3D, opt-in.** Cameras and imported scenes (OBJ, glTF, USD), instanced meshes and GPU-culled worlds, grass and an ocean, physically based materials with the full map set, glass with dispersion, area lights, image-based lighting and a procedural sky. Shadows, ray-traced reflections, caustics, global illumination, atmosphere, temporal anti-aliasing, motion blur, and lens flare. A 2D sketch never pays for any of it. [3D](Docs/3D/README.md).
- **The world as input.** Nineteen on-device Vision trackers over the camera or any video, depth from a video model, screen and window capture, and video playback. CSV, JSON, and SVG import, live and pushed data feeds, game controllers, and an iPhone over USB as a LiDAR, motion, body, face, and hand sensor. [Vision](Docs/Vision/Vision.md), [Data](Docs/Helpers/Data.md), [The phone](Docs/3D/Phone.md).
- **Sound.** FFT analysis with beat detection, listening for words and named sounds, and a synthesizer the sketch plays. Voice presets, physical models, sampled instruments, wavetables, a patchable graph, effects chains, a place in the 3D scene, and algorithmic composition, all carried into an exported video. [Audio](Docs/Helpers/Audio.md), [Synthesis](Docs/Helpers/Synthesis.md).
- **Integration.** MIDI, OSC, a network tempo session, serial, Bluetooth Low Energy, DMX and LED mapping, and show lasers. Syphon out and in, a virtual camera, haptics, a remote surface for a phone, and a room several machines join to draw one piece on one clock. [Integration](Docs/Integration/README.md).
- **Output.** Headless stills and deterministic sequences, MP4 and GIF with slow motion, SVG and PDF with hatched fills, G-code, and print separations with press proofing. STL, OBJ, and 3MF for printing, USDZ and spatial video, a path-traced export, live recording with sound, and an accessibility layer for color vision and motion. [Export](Docs/Output/Export.md).
- **Running in the system.** A finished piece as a signed Mac app, a screen saver, the desktop wallpaper, a menu-bar strip, or an installation that stays up for a week. [A sketch as an app](Docs/Output/App.md), [Installation](Docs/Output/Installation.md).

### The tools

- **The `ollin` command.** One loose `.swift` file is a whole sketch, run from any directory. `ollin new` writes a starter, `ollin install` puts the command on PATH, and `ollin check` compiles a shader on this machine's GPU. `ollin docs` and `ollin examples` read the reference and the examples in the terminal. [Single-file sketches](Docs/Tools/SingleFile.md), [The reference offline](Docs/Tools/Reference.md).
- **OllinLive.** Save the file and the running window hot-swaps the sketch, with the clock carried across. `@Param` properties appear as typed inspector controls, a timeline records their automation, and Command-drag moves, resizes, or turns a shape by editing the source. `⌘]` and `⌘[` put it in front of or behind its neighbor. [The parameter timeline](Docs/Tools/Timeline.md), [Dragging a shape](Docs/Tools/DragToEdit.md).
- **OllinLiveCoding.** The performance host: the sketch fills the window, the code rides over it as translucent text, and Command-Return recompiles the buffer mid-motion. [Live coding](Docs/Tools/LiveCoding.md).
- **The project generator.** `ollin new` and `ollin generate` write a ready-to-run project from ten templates or from any example. They also bring a GLSL fragment shader or a glTF or USD scene over as source, and scaffold an extension library other sketches import. [Project generator](Docs/Tools/ProjectGenerator.md).
- **The examples.** 494 example sketches in their own package, browsable in one gallery window, including twelve recreations after eight artists. [Examples](Examples/README.md).

### The documentation

- The reference under [Docs/](Docs/README.md), about two hundred pages, with a short concept page for each idea the reference assumes.
- The [Guide](Guide/README.md), thirty-two chapters and four appendices: a practical introduction to creative coding taught through Ollin.
- For contributors, [CAPABILITIES.md](CAPABILITIES.md) inventories every capability's invariants, [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) explains the internals, and [ATTRIBUTION.md](ATTRIBUTION.md) credits the influences and the techniques.

### Requirements

- macOS 26 or later, a Metal-capable GPU, and a Swift 6 toolchain. Apple platforms only, by design: iOS and visionOS are future targets, and Linux, Windows, and the web are out of scope.

### Versioning from here

- Tags begin at 0.1.0. A breaking change or a new feature bumps the minor, a fix bumps the patch, and each release names its breaking renames in this file. Deprecation shims and a settled surface arrive at 1.0. Until then, pin `.upToNextMinor(from: "0.1.0")`.
