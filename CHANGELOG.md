# Changelog

Notable changes to Ollin, newest first. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/). Version numbers follow semantic versioning at major zero: a breaking change or a new feature bumps the minor, and a fix bumps the patch. Each release names its breaking renames here, so pin `.upToNextMinor` and read the entry before you move up.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **An app for the phone, from the generator.** `ollin new <Name> --kind ios-app` writes a sketch as an iPhone and iPad app: the sketch, a host that owns the entry point, the property list, and the `project.yml` that `xcodegen` turns into the Xcode project, with the framework reached by path. `--team <id>` (or `OLLIN_TEAM`) sets the signing team; left off, Xcode asks, and the README says where to keep the answer. The generator window offers the kind from its menu with a field for the team. A capability that lives on the Mac (Syphon, screen capture, the virtual camera, the phone link) is refused with its reason. `ProjectRequest` gains `team`, `Capability` gains `staysOnTheDesk`, and `ProjectGeneratorError` gains `staysOnTheDesk`.
- **The logo.** Ollin has a mark: the day sign's crossing bands with an eye at the center and four marks at the cardinal points, in plain geometry. `Logo/` holds it as SVG in a full form and a small form, each for a light and a dark ground, with a README naming the geometry and the colors. The README opens with it, and the website wears it in the bar, on the front page, and as the favicon, drawn in the page's own ink so it follows the reader's color scheme.
- **A tempo, and note lengths with names.** `Tempo` is beats per minute with a `beatsPerBar`: `tempo.beats(at: time)` is what a `StepCounter` wants each frame, `tempo.seconds(of: .eighth)` what a synth's `for:` wants, and `seconds(beats:)`, `seconds(bars:)`, `bars(at:)`, and `secondsPerBeat` read the rest. `NoteLength` names the lengths on a stave, `.whole` down to `.thirtySecond`, with `.dotted` and `.triplet` deriving the others. A number stands in for either, so `tempo: 120` and `length: 0.5` read as before. Both are parameters: a tempo is a slider in beats per minute that a MIDI knob or an OSC address binds to the way it binds to a number, and a note length is a menu of the named lengths.
- **Tensegrities.** `Tensegrity` describes a structure of struts held apart by cables, with three balanced forms ready-made: `prism(struts:)`, the six-strut `icosahedron(strutLength:)`, and a `tower(levels:)` of stacked prisms. `imbalance` says whether an arrangement of your own can hold. `World3D.addTensegrity(_:at:)` gives one weight, so it falls, lands on its own cables, and stands, and `drawTensegrity(_:)` draws it where it is. A new `.cable` joint kind holds two anchors no farther apart than a length and lets them come closer, the rope beside `.distance`'s rod. A saved world now carries its tensegrities back as structures (snapshot format 5; files written before it still load).
- **The weather outside.** `Weather(at:)`, `Weather(latitude:longitude:)`, and `Weather(in: "Oaxaca")` read the current conditions over a place every fifteen minutes, as `temperature`, `humidity`, `cloudCover`, `precipitation`, `snowfall`, the wind, `pressure`, `isDay`, `condition` (the sky in a word), and the day's `sunrise` and `sunset`, in plain units, with the feed's own manners: nothing throws, a failure keeps the last reading, and an export reads once. `Place.sun(at:)` puts the sun in the sky from the place and the clock, with no network. The conditions come from Open-Meteo, which needs no key and is free for non-commercial use. Example `Data/Outside`; `Docs/Helpers/Weather.md`; Guide Chapter 9.
- **Strokes and fills as points on the web page.** `--export-web` now carries a stroke as the points and the style the sketch gave (its weight, join, cap, a width or a color per point when a profile or a gradient varies them, and the transform it was drawn under), and a polygon or a shape as its contours, and the page expands them itself through the framework's own fringe expander and tessellator compiled to WebAssembly, so the vertices on the page are the Mac's own code's. A dense line drawing now weighs its points rather than its bands: the six heaviest pattern examples, which their vertices had put at 51 to 325 MB over four frames and the weight limit had refused, cross at 386 KB to 8.7 MB and read within a mean of 0.14 levels of the Mac. Outline text and a fill under a gradient still travel as vertices. The module weighs 97 KB on a page and travels only when the page has points to expand; a refusal past the weight limit names it and the new `strokes and fills as points` part.
- **Parquet deformations.** `parquetDeformation(columns:rows:from:to:sweep:)` lays a tiling whose tile changes shape as you read across it: a square at one edge of the sheet becomes an interlocking key at the other, and no piece comes apart on the way, because the geometry lives on the lattice's shared edges rather than on the tiles. An edge is a `Profile` in its own frame (`.straight`, `.tooth`, `.zigzag`, `.wave`, `.bump`, or `.custom`), and the blend between the two named profiles is exact at both ends, so a square tooth keeps square corners. `Sweep` runs the change left to right, downward, diagonally, or out from the center; the closure form takes a point and answers how far along the run it sits, so the drift can follow noise, an image, or a front moving with `time`. Read `tiles` for the interlocking pieces, each carrying the amount it was built at, or `edges` for line-work that visits every edge exactly once. [Parquet deformations](Docs/Drawing/ParquetDeformation.md).
- **A weight limit on the web page.** `--export-web` refuses a page past 25 MB instead of writing it: the message says what the page would have weighed, which part of it is heaviest (the stroke and fill vertices, the shapes and passes, the pictures, the atlas pages, or the shaders and the player), whether that part is stored for every recorded frame, so a lower `--fps` or a shorter `--seconds` would take most of it away, or travels once, so it would not, that the sketch exports as video, and the `--max-page-size` that writes the page anyway (`0` lifts the limit). A drawing dense enough is refused during the recording, at its first frame or within its first few, from what the frames seen so far must put on the page, so the export never holds the frames a page would not get. In code, `maxBytes:` on `OllinApp.web(of:)` and `exportWeb`, and a `WebWeightRefusal` carrying the numbers. Two things got lighter on the way: a sampled column's range and index travel as binary rather than JSON text, twelve bytes a column, which takes a moving line drawing's page down by about a third; and the page is assembled in one pass rather than one copy per slot, which took a 400 MB page from two minutes to seconds.
- **Parameters as controls on the web page.** `--export-web` now offers a sketch's `@Param`s on the page. After the recording, each parameter is probed on a fresh sketch at a few other settings, and one whose every effect on the picture moves along a line in it becomes a control: a slider, a stepper, a switch, a color well, or a field per part, under the canvas in the standalone page and through the handle (`params`, `get`, `set`, `reset`) in the inline fragment, so a page can set a sketch's colors to its own theme. Moving a control draws what the Mac draws at that setting. A parameter that changes what is drawn, moves the picture some other way, acts together with another, is a menu or text, or is driven by a formula stays at its recorded value, and the exporter names it and says why. `--no-controls` skips the probe. [Parameters as controls](Docs/Output/Web.md#parameters-as-controls).
- **Fields on the web page.** `--export-web` now carries a composed field (`drawSDF`) as its program, walked on the page by the same machine over the framework's own arithmetic carried to GLSL, and a raymarched field (`drawSDF3D`) with the camera it was seen through and the lights it was lit by: directional, point, and spot lights, the ambient, the `castShadows()` self-shadow, the four shading models with their finishes, a gradient fill, and a `raymarchResolution` below one traced at the Mac's own fraction. An environment, fog, an area light, a shaped light, the traced and screen-space passes, and a glass, coated, sheened, thin-film, brushed, or scattering finish still stop the export by name. [Fields on the page](Docs/Output/Web.md#fields-on-the-page).
- **A lens as a field of view.** `.focalLength(35)` is the vertical angle a 35 mm lens sees on a full-frame camera, so `fieldOfView:` takes the lens's own number beside `.degrees(_:)`; pass `sensorHeight:` for a smaller format.
- **Ramp stops as values, and a ramp reversed.** `Ramp(stops: [Ramp.Stop])` takes typed stops beside the tuple list, and `ramp.reversed` runs a ramp the other way, a hard edge keeping its colors in mirrored order.
- **Depth of field from light.** `LineSpray` renders an array of `SprayLine`s (3D segments with a color at each end, an intensity, and a weight) as depth of field the way a lens makes it: every frame scatters passes of points along the lines on the GPU, pushes each into a `Bokeh` ball that grows with its distance from the plane of focus, projects it through the sketch's camera, and adds it into a running mean, so bokeh emerges as the picture converges and nothing is blurred afterward. `makeLineSpray(_:sampling:passesPerFrame:bokeh:)`, `drawLineSpray(_:)`, and `developed(exposure:ground:)` to print it; moving the camera or the lens restarts the average. The example `Rendering/LineSpray` is a whole scene through it. [Depth of field from light](Docs/Drawing/DepthOfField.md).
- **A running mean of light.** `makeAccumulator()` / `withAccumulator(_:passes:)` / `Accumulator`: a layer that keeps the sum of every pass drawn into it in single-precision float and the number of passes beside it, and hands back the mean as `image`, so a picture built from faint samples converges instead of brightening without end. `reset()` starts it over, `sum` and `passes` are there for a shader of your own, and `Filter.develop(exposure:ground:)` prints accumulated light the reference way: exposure, a Reinhard roll-off, the ground added after the curve as a display value. `makeRenderTarget(precision:)` and `makeFeedback(precision:)` take `LayerPrecision.float32` for a layer whose values are sums. [Accumulation](Docs/Drawing/Accumulation.md#accumulator).
- **Particles as light.** `drawParticles(_:style: .light)` deposits each particle's `color × alpha × area` as linear light with no perceptual remap, the same wherever it lands on the pixel grid, and draws a particle at or under one pixel as a single texel, so a million one-pixel points cost a million fragments rather than twenty-five million. The default `.marks` style is unchanged. [The light particle style](Docs/Drawing/DepthOfField.md#light).
- **A kernel that sees the camera.** `Camera3D.viewMatrix`, `projectionMatrix(aspect:)`, `viewProjectionMatrix(aspect:)`, and `matrices(aspect:)` are public; `ComputeParams.append(camera:aspect:)` packs them as an `OllinCameraMatrices`, `cameraParams()` does it for the active camera, and the shader library's `ollin_project` / `ollin_project_eye` project a world or camera-space point to canvas points inside a kernel. `ballSample(float3)` joins `discSample` in the library: a point uniform over the unit ball's volume. [Projecting through the camera](Docs/Shaders/Compute.md#camera).
- **Settled frames in an export.** `--settle N` (and `OllinApp.exportSettle`) draws each written frame N times with the clock held and writes the last, so a picture that converges over frames, an `Accumulator`'s mean or a `LineSpray` through a turning camera, settles for every frame of a still, a sequence, a video, or a GIF. The recipe records it; a take and made frames refuse it. [Settled frames](Docs/Output/Export.md#settled-frames).
- **Compute binds up to ten buffers.** `compute(_:buffers:count:params:)` hands a kernel any number of `ComputeBuffer`s of any element types at indices 0…9, and `compute(_:reading:writing:)` now takes two element types, so a kernel can read a static geometry buffer and write an `OllinParticle` buffer without packing one into the other. `ComputeBindable` is public. `Color.linearRGBA` is public too, the radiance a light particle carries.

- **Fractal fields.** Three fractals join the raymarched field as leaves: `SDF3D.mandelbulb(power:iterations:radius:)`, `.mengerSponge(iterations:size:)`, and `.mandelbox(scale:iterations:size:)`. They melt, carve, mirror, and repeat like any other leaf, and the example `3D/Raymarching/RaymarchedFractals` turns each one's own dial.
- **The sketch on the phone.** A sketch runs on an iPhone or iPad as an app, and `ollin phone Sketch.swift` puts it there and installs it again on every save, its clock and parameters carried across, with the parameters live on the Mac. `SketchRunner.beginInstallation` is public, so an app of your own can turn the checkpoint on. [The sketch on the phone](Docs/Tools/OnThePhone.md).
- **A Web example group.** `Examples/Web/BreathingRing` draws the ring of breathing circles the site opens on as a sketch: a sixty-second loop that wraps without a seam, its ink and paper as parameters so a page can set its own theme.
- **A sketch as a web page.** `--export-web` records what a sketch draws over a duration and writes a page that plays it back in a browser, one self-contained file or, with `--inline`, a fragment for a page of your own, the shapes drawn by the framework's own shader carried from Metal to GLSL. A sketch that declares `loopDuration` records one lap and the page wraps it without a seam, and the lap's motions are fitted to the sines they are made of, so the page evaluates them rather than reading them back. A parameter driven by a formula crosses as the formula, evaluated live on the page's clock and pointer. What fits neither travels as 16-bit samples. The analytic shapes cross; anything else stops the export and names the call. [A sketch as a web page](Docs/Output/Web.md).
- **Layers and shaders on the web page.** `--export-web` now carries the layered effects: layers drawn with `withTarget` at their own resolution, every `Generator`, every `Filter` and `Combine` that is one shader pass (plus `.gaussianBlur` and `.bloom` as passes of the page's own), lookup filters with their strips, `postProcess`, a user `Shader` as a generator, filter, or combine with `info.time` and `info.mouse` live on the page, compiled `Visual` chains, and a `Feedback` layer or a single-field `SimField` keeping its state on the page. The framework's own fragments cross to GLSL with everything they reach, and only the ones a recording runs land on the page. The solves, the ladders, and the simulations with pipelines of their own still leave as video, and the message names the one that stopped the export.
- **Pictures, atlas text, and gradients on the web page.** `--export-web` now carries a picture drawn with `drawImage` (a loaded PNG, JPEG, GIF, or WebP as the file itself, anything else as a PNG of its pixels, once however many frames draw it), text through the glyph atlas as its quads over the font's page, and a gradient on an analytic shape from the strip the Mac bakes it into. A picture that is a live texture (a camera, a video, a compute texture) still leaves as video. The page draws its first frame once the browser has decoded the pictures, and its handle gains `ready`.
- **Strokes and fills on the web page.** `--export-web` now carries every stroke (`drawLine`, `drawPolyline`, `drawBezier`, `drawCurve`, a shape's outline, an elliptical or full-turn `drawArc`) and every fill the triangle path tessellates (`drawPolygon` from points, `drawShape` with its holes, `drawCurve` closed, text in the default outline mode), as the vertices the Mac would have drawn, a fill's edge anti-aliased through the same four samples the Mac uses, and a blend mode on shapes, strokes, and fills. A `Batch` recorded from them replays under its transform. A caption no longer stops a sketch; a picture, atlas text, and a clip still do. A dense line drawing makes a heavy page for now, and the exporter prints the size.
- **The website.** `ollin site` writes the README, the Guide, the reference, and every example with its source out as a static website, the pages rendered from the same files `ollin docs` reads, with a workflow that publishes it to GitHub Pages. [The reference offline](Docs/Tools/Reference.md#in-a-browser).

### Changed

- **Note lengths and tempos are typed.** `Note.length` and `Sonification.noteLength` are `NoteLength`s, and `Note.seconds(at:)`, `Synth.play(_:tempo:)`, and `Synth.play(_:step:tempo:)` take a `Tempo`. A literal compiles as it did; a `Double` variable becomes `Tempo(bpm)` or `NoteLength(beats:)`.
- **The reader-facing pages in a plain register.** Every reference page, the README, the roadmap, the example lists, and the concept pages are rewritten sentence by sentence: one idea per sentence, plain words, active voice, no em dashes, the same facts. Along the way the facts a reader would act on were checked against the source, and about a dozen stale API names, several wrong counts, and five figures whose caption disagreed with the picture are corrected. `Tone`'s lighter `smoothing` default now says why on the audio page.
- **The site opens on the sketch.** The front page `ollin site` writes now plays `Examples/Web/BreathingRing` as the web page `--export-web` records for it, in place of a hand-written copy of the ring: its ink and paper are set to the site's own colors and follow a change of scheme, and a reader who asked for less motion sees its first frame, still. `Scripts/site-hero.sh` records the page into the site's own source, and the preflight reruns it when anything it carries changes.
- **The live hosts compile the sketch optimized.** `OllinLive`, `OllinLiveCoding`, `OllinRun`, and the gallery now compile a sketch with `-O`, so a sketch that does real CPU work every frame runs under the live window at the speed `swift run -c release` gives it, rather than the several-times-slower plain compile `swiftc` defaults to. `--no-optimize` compiles it plain when you are debugging the sketch itself: asserts fire, and a crash names every frame.
- **Recording and frame sharing no longer draw the frame twice.** A live take (`startRecording()`, ⌘⇧R, `--record`), a Syphon feed, the virtual camera, an LED map, and any `SketchExtension` that asks for the rendered frame now receive the frame the window shows, tone-mapped once more into a canvas-sized texture inside the frame's own command buffer and delivered once the GPU has finished it, in frame order. A lit 3D scene that recorded at 19 fps records at its unarmed 46; a shared feed carries lit meshes and effects it used to lack. The frame arrives a refresh or so after `afterFrame` rather than during it, and `wantsRenderedFrame` / `wantsRenderedTexture` are read once per frame. A window larger than the canvas is scaled down into the take, a smaller one draws the frame at the canvas size while a hook is armed, and the 3D ground grid leaves the window while one is.
- **A typed box for bounds.** `Box3` replaces the `(min: Vector3, max: Vector3)` tuple everywhere it appeared: `Scene.bounds`, `Metaballs.bounds`, `ShadowArt.bounds`, `PhoneSceneMesh.bounds`, `PhonePlanes.bounds`, and the `in:` box of `isosurface` and `shadowArt`. Reading `.min` and `.max` is unchanged; a tuple literal becomes `Box3(min:max:)`, and `center`, `size`, `longestSide`, `padded(by:)`, and `union(_:)` come with it.
- The `Rendering/DepthOfField` example runs on the accumulating pipeline: a million samples a pass deposited as light into a running mean that converges, with the focus, the lens, the color shift, and the passes per frame as parameters, instead of a `noClear` pile that brightened forever.

### Fixed

- **A shader beside a phone sketch reached the phone as a library, not as source.** `ollin phone` listed a `.metal` sibling in the resources build phase, which Xcode compiles; the framework reads a shader's source at run time, so the file was useless there. It now rides through a copy-files phase and arrives as the text it was.
- **A test parked a worker on the environment download.** The remote-HDRI cache had only a blocking download, right on the export path's main thread and wrong from a test body, where it held one of the concurrency pool's few threads; it was the other parked thread in every sample of the CI wedge. The cache now has an `async` `download(_:)` that suspends instead, the tests use it, and preflight refuses the blocking form under Tests.
- **A picture could park the whole run.** An image's texture was built through the synchronous MetalKit loader, which decodes on a dispatch worker and waits on its own semaphore; with every worker already blocked, as a busy test run manages, the main thread waited forever, which is what the CI watchdog sampled on 2026-09-06 (three failed Build runs in five). Textures are now decoded on the calling thread and their mip chains blitted on a command buffer, so no worker is needed. The pixels are unchanged: the whole snapshot suite and the figure probe compare clean.
- **A translucent PNG composites at its own alpha.** ImageIO decodes a PNG with alpha straight, and the image pipeline blends premultiplied, so a translucent pixel of a loaded PNG drew too bright; the texture is now premultiplied on upload, as an image painted in memory already was.

- The Accumulation and HDR pages described a sum on a `noClear` canvas plus a tone map as the basis of the sandpainting look and left out the divide by the number of passes; both now describe the running mean and point at `Accumulator` and `Filter.develop`.
- The Compute reference said a kernel binds its own buffers at indices 0…9 while the entry points reached two of one type; the `buffers:` form now matches the claim.
- A raymarched field under an orthographic camera drew a soft halo at its silhouette and lost gaps narrower than the halo; the silhouette anti-aliasing now sizes its pixel cone for an orthographic projection.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-09-02

The first tagged release. Ollin grew in one repository from May 2026 to this tag, and that history is not itemized here. This entry says what the release contains, area by area, with a link to the reference for each. The [README](README.md#whats-in-it) carries the fuller catalog, and the [Guide](Guide/README.md) teaches it in order. Read this entry as a table of contents rather than a list of changes.

### The framework

- **Drawing.** A p5-style API over a Metal core: `setup()` and `draw()`, and bare calls like `drawCircle` and `background`. `draw()` runs at the display's refresh rate from the first line, so motion is the default. Some thirty analytic shapes, curved paths, concave and holed fills, variable-width strokes, marks that follow the hand, shape booleans and offsets, gradients, blend modes, clipping, accumulation, and retained batches. Every frame composites in linear light with HDR tone mapping and analytic anti-aliasing. [Drawing](Docs/Drawing/Drawing.md).
- **Text and color.** Bitmap, outline, and single-line plotter fonts through one `drawText`, in any script, in columns, justified, along a path, or as vector shapes. OKLab mixing, spectral paint mixing, palettes and colormaps, color vision simulation, and wide-gamut and HDR output. [Text](Docs/Drawing/Text.md), [Color](Docs/Drawing/Color.md).
- **Shapes that merge.** SDF combinators in 2D and in raymarched 3D: smooth union, subtraction, morphing, domain mirror, tile, and radial repeat, and a clay-like `sculpt { }` block. [Combinators](Docs/Drawing/Combinators.md).
- **Generative technique.** Sixty-six classic techniques as first-class helpers, from Poisson-disk scatter and L-systems to Wave Function Collapse, strange attractors, marching cubes, paper marbling, and terrain erosion. [Generators](Docs/Generators/README.md).
- **Layers, effects, and shaders.** Off-screen layers with fifty-plus GPU filters, two-layer combines, feedback, simulation fields, and a compose DSL. Measured distance fields, light with shadows in a flat sketch, the frequency domain, and summed-area averages. Your own `shade(uv, info)` fragment function, inline or in a hot-reloading `.metal` file, with errors reported at your own line numbers. Chainable `Visual`s when you would rather skip the Metal. [Effects](Docs/Drawing/Effects.md), [Shaders](Docs/Shaders/Shaders.md).
- **Motion and parameters.** Frame-rate-independent easing and springs, keyframe timelines, seeded variations, and record and replay. `@Param` properties become typed inspector controls, persist across reloads, and can follow a curve or a formula. [Sketch](Docs/Core/Sketch.md), [Automation](Docs/Core/Automation.md), [Variations](Docs/Core/Variations.md).
- **GPU compute and simulation.** Compute kernels with a million particles a frame, and a neighbor-search primitive under artificial life and SPH fluids. Ping-pong texture simulations, steering crowds at GPU scale, populations that evolve, and chaotic systems. [Compute](Docs/Shaders/Compute.md), [Simulation](Docs/Simulation/README.md).
- **Physics.** A stepped `World` with Verlet particles and springs, Box2D bodies and joints in 2D, and Jolt-backed 3D bodies, joints, characters, vehicles, ragdolls, cloth, ropes, and water. [Physics](Docs/Simulation/Physics.md), [Physics in 3D](Docs/Simulation/Physics3D.md).
- **3D, opt-in.** Cameras and imported scenes (OBJ, glTF, USD), instanced meshes and GPU-culled worlds, grass and an ocean, physically based materials with the full map set, glass with dispersion, area lights, image-based lighting and a procedural sky. Shadows, ray-traced reflections, caustics, global illumination, atmosphere, temporal anti-aliasing, motion blur, and lens flare. A 2D sketch never pays for any of it. [3D](Docs/3D/README.md).
- **The world as input.** Nineteen on-device Vision trackers over the camera or any video, depth from a video model, screen and window capture, and video playback. CSV, JSON, and SVG import, live and pushed data feeds, game controllers, and an iPhone over USB as a LiDAR, motion, body, face, and hand sensor. [Vision](Docs/Vision/Vision.md), [Data](Docs/Helpers/Data.md), [The phone](Docs/3D/Phone.md).
- **Sound.** FFT analysis with beat detection, listening for words and named sounds, and a synthesizer the sketch plays. Voice presets, physical models, sampled instruments, wavetables, a patchable graph, effects chains, a place in the 3D scene, and algorithmic composition, all carried into an exported video. [Audio](Docs/Helpers/Audio.md), [Synthesis](Docs/Helpers/Synthesis.md).
- **Integration.** MIDI, OSC, a network tempo session, serial, Bluetooth Low Energy, DMX and LED mapping, and show lasers. Syphon out and in, a virtual camera, haptics, a remote surface for a phone, and a room several machines join to draw one piece on one clock. [Integration](Docs/Integration/README.md).
- **Output.** Headless stills and deterministic sequences, MP4 and GIF with slow motion, SVG and PDF with hatched fills, G-code, and print separations with press proofing. STL, OBJ, and 3MF for printing, USDZ and spatial video, a path-traced export, live recording with sound, and an accessibility layer for color vision and motion. [Export](Docs/Output/Export.md).
- **Running in the system.** A finished piece as a signed Mac app, a screen saver, the desktop wallpaper, a menu-bar strip, or an installation that stays up for a week. [A sketch as an app](Docs/Output/App.md), [Installation](Docs/Output/Installation.md).

### The tools

- **The `ollin` command.** One loose `.swift` file is a whole sketch, run from any directory. `ollin new` writes a starter, `ollin install` puts the command on PATH, and `ollin check` compiles a shader on this machine's GPU. `ollin docs` and `ollin examples` read the reference and the examples in the terminal. [Single-file sketches](Docs/Tools/SingleFile.md), [The reference offline](Docs/Tools/Reference.md).
- **OllinLive.** Save the file and the running window hot-swaps the sketch, with the clock carried across. `@Param` properties appear as typed inspector controls, a timeline records their automation, and Command-drag moves, resizes, or turns a shape by editing the source. `⌘]` and `⌘[` put it in front of or behind its neighbor. [The parameter timeline](Docs/Tools/Timeline.md), [Dragging a shape](Docs/Tools/DragToEdit.md).
- **OllinLiveCoding.** The performance host: the sketch fills the window, the code rides over it as translucent text, and Command-Return recompiles the buffer mid-motion. [Live coding](Docs/Tools/LiveCoding.md).
- **The project generator.** `ollin new` and `ollin generate` write a ready-to-run project from ten templates or from any example. They also bring a GLSL fragment shader or a glTF or USD scene over as source, and scaffold an extension library other sketches import. [Project generator](Docs/Tools/ProjectGenerator.md).
- **The examples.** 494 example sketches in their own package, browsable in one gallery window, including twelve recreations after eight artists. [Examples](Examples/README.md).

### The documentation

- The reference under [Docs/](Docs/README.md), about two hundred pages, with a short concept page for each idea the reference assumes.
- The [Guide](Guide/README.md), thirty-two chapters and four appendices: a practical introduction to creative coding taught through Ollin.
- For contributors, [CAPABILITIES.md](CAPABILITIES.md) inventories every capability's invariants, [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) explains the internals, and [ATTRIBUTION.md](ATTRIBUTION.md) credits the influences and the techniques.

### Requirements

- macOS 26 or later, a Metal-capable GPU, and a Swift 6 toolchain. Apple platforms only, by design: iOS and visionOS are future targets, and Linux, Windows, and the web are out of scope.

### Versioning from here
