## Recreations

Sketches that **recreate the work of past computer artists**: rebuilding a piece to learn from it, and seeing what a modern framework brings to it.

The idea comes from the [**Recreating the Past**](https://sfpc.io/recreatingthepast-spring2020/) class at the [School for Poetic Computation (SFPC)](https://sfpc.io), taught by Zach Lieberman: *"By recreating historic works, we also investigate what modern tools and approaches have to offer."* The class treats history as something you take part in, not only something you consume.

These differ from the rest of `Examples/`: the others are a *feature* learning path ("how do I use X?"); recreations are organized **by artist** and ask "how was this made, and how does it feel to rebuild it?" They're maintained and compile-tested like every example, and each one tends to push the framework, and a recreation that's awkward to write tells us what to build next.

Each sketch is a **recreation and homage** made after the artist. It isn't a reproduction of a specific work, and it isn't affiliated with or endorsed by the artist or their estate. Folders are named for the artist; each has a `README.md` introducing them and where to learn more.

### Artists

- [**Vera Molnár**](VeraMolnar/). Order and disorder in algorithmic geometry.
- [**Bridget Riley**](BridgetRiley/). Black-and-white Op art, with optical movement from precise geometry.
- [**Osamu Sato**](OsamuSato/). Surreal totems and glyphs built from basic shapes, in bold black and white with red.
- [**Nick Cave**](NickCave/). The Soundsuits: wearable sculptures that conceal the body and sound as it moves, recreated live on the streamed skeleton.
- [**Jared Tarbell**](JaredTarbell/). Emergent city maps grown from colliding cracks, shaded with sand-grain washes.
