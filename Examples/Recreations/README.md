These sketches **recreate the work of past computer artists**. Each one rebuilds a piece to learn from it, and shows what a modern framework brings to it.

The idea comes from the [**Recreating the Past**](https://sfpc.io/recreatingthepast-spring2020/) class at the [School for Poetic Computation (SFPC)](https://sfpc.io), taught by Zach Lieberman. The class states its aim this way: *"By recreating historic works, we also investigate what modern tools and approaches have to offer"*. So the class treats history as something you take part in, not only something you consume.

These sketches differ from the rest of `Examples/`. The other examples are a *feature* learning path, so each one answers the question "how do I use X?" for one feature. Recreations are organized **by artist** instead, and each one asks "how was this made, and how does it feel to rebuild it?" about one piece. They are maintained and compile-tested like every example. Each one also tends to push the framework, because a recreation that is awkward to write tells us what to build next.

Each sketch is a **recreation and homage** made after the artist. It is not a reproduction of a specific work, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by the artist or their estate. Folders are named for the artist, and each folder has a `README.md` that introduces the artist and says where to learn more.

### Artists

- [**Georg Nees**](GeorgNees/). The first exhibited computer art, and the plate where a ruled grid falls apart into gravel.
- [**Frieder Nake**](FriederNake/). Horizontal bands with buckled edges, and lines, triangles, and circles set into them by chance, after the plotter drawing that became an icon of the first computer art.
- [**Vera Molnár**](VeraMolnar/). Order and disorder in algorithmic geometry.
- [**Bridget Riley**](BridgetRiley/). Black-and-white Op art, where precise geometry produces optical movement.
- [**Osamu Sato**](OsamuSato/). Surreal totems and glyphs built from basic shapes, in bold black and white with red.
- [**Nick Cave**](NickCave/). The Soundsuits: wearable sculptures that hide the body and make sound as it moves, recreated live on the streamed skeleton.
- [**Jared Tarbell**](JaredTarbell/). Emergent city maps grown from colliding cracks, shaded with sand-grain washes.
- [**Manuel Felguérez**](ManuelFelguerez/). One form-idea cut from the circle, the triangle and the square, carried from the painting to the relief to a standing piece and back; a machine that composes in the artist's own geometric language and weighs what it makes; and one of its designs raised into a lacquered relief under a raking light.
- [**Manfred Mohr**](ManfredMohr/). A cube taken apart and used as an alphabet, and walks through its four-dimensional counterpart.
- [**Josef Albers**](JosefAlbers/). Three or four nested squares set low in the panel, and a new set of colors on them every few seconds, each chosen so the squares change one another.
- [**John Whitney**](JohnWhitney/). Points on concentric circles, each turning at a whole-number multiple of the first, so the fan gathers into a star at every simple fraction of the cycle and comes back to one spoke at the end of it.
- [**Sol LeWitt**](SolLeWitt/). Instructions written for other people to draw: fifty random points all connected by straight lines, carried out wall after wall, and the two-part combinations of arcs, circles, and grids from his compass alphabet.
- [**Casey Reas**](CaseyReas/). A written instruction that gives elements their behaviors and leaves the picture to what they do to each other: circles whose meetings are recorded as lines on a surface that keeps them all, and lines whose touching pairs carry a plane that comes up while they stay together.
- [**Vicente Rojo**](VicenteRojo/). The letter T painted only as the shadow it casts, a wall of them painted over one at a time, and rain falling across a square as a grid of small marks in diagonal streaks.
- [**Ryszard Winiarski**](RyszardWiniarski/). A grid whose every square a coin or a die decided, painted from a drawn corner one area after another with the rule written beside it, and black and white runs whose lengths are the sums of two dice, the throws written under them.
- [**Hassan Sharif**](HassanSharif/). One table of picked numbers drawn three times across a sheet, as the dots a line touches, as the line, and as the line inside its square, with the draft paper of numbers hung beside it; and sheets of small angular lines through numbered crossings, one of them chosen and painted as a black band across a red canvas.
- [**Owen Schuh**](OwenSchuh/). Two pages of a notebook where a digit is a color: the table of fractions woven as cloth and counted along its diagonals, and a list of numbers whose diagonal builds the number that is on no line of it.
- [**Monir Shahroudy Farmanfarmaian**](MonirFarmanfarmaian/). A family of polygons cut from the circle, triangle to decagon, each set piece by piece in tilted mirror and painted glass and lit by a moving lamp; and a maze of spiral channels painted on the back of a pane, lines first and silver last, then turned over.
- [**Gego**](Gego/). A net of irregular triangles tied by hand, hung from the ceiling at a few points and left to gravity, drawn as wire that thins as it goes away from you; and a drawing without paper, a bent lattice hanging a little off the wall whose shadow under a crossing lamp is the drawing.
- [**Vladimir Bonačić**](VladimirBonacic/). A square of lamps lit a pattern at a time by a walk through a finite field, the pattern read and set in binary on its back; and a frieze of eighteen lamp panels across a store front at night, stepping through every state of an 18-bit field and sending them to a real wall.
