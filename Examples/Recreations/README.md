# Recreations

Sketches that **recreate the work of past computer artists** — rebuilding a piece to learn from it, and seeing what a modern framework brings to it.

The idea comes from the [**Recreating the Past**](https://sfpc.io/recreatingthepast-spring2020/) class at the [School for Poetic Computation (SFPC)](https://sfpc.io), taught by Zach Lieberman: *"By recreating historic works, we also investigate what modern tools and approaches have to offer."* History as something you *do*, not just consume.

These differ from the rest of `Examples/`: the others are a *feature* learning path ("how do I use X?"); recreations are organized **by artist** and ask "how was this made, and how does it feel to rebuild it?" They're maintained and compile-tested like every example, and each one tends to push the framework — a recreation that's awkward to write tells us what to build next.

Each sketch is a **recreation / homage** — *after* the artist, not a reproduction of a specific work, and not affiliated with or endorsed by the artist or their estate. Folders are named for the artist; each has a `README.md` introducing them and where to learn more.

## Artists

- [**Vera Molnár**](VeraMolnar/) — order and disorder in algorithmic geometry.
- [**Bridget Riley**](BridgetRiley/) — black-and-white Op art; optical movement from precise geometry.
