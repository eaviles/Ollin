### His life

**Nick Cave** is an American artist who trained as a fiber artist and a dancer. He grew up in Fulton, Missouri, one of seven boys. His grandparents made quilts and furniture, and he has said he learned to make things out of whatever was around. "When you're raised by a single mother with six brothers and lots of hand-me-downs, you have to figure out how to make those clothes your own," he told The New York Times in 2009.

He studied fiber at the Kansas City Art Institute, graduating in 1982, and in those years took summer classes with Alvin Ailey in New York, where he learned to dance. He finished a master's degree at Cranbrook Academy of Art in 1989. In 1990 he came to Chicago to teach fashion design at the School of the Art Institute, and he has lived and taught there since.

The Soundsuits led to larger works. In 2013 students of The Ailey School danced thirty life-size horses through Grand Central Terminal (*HEARD•NY*). Mosaics of nearly fifty Soundsuits line a passage of the New York subway (*Each One, Every One, Equal All*, 2021 to 2022). His first retrospective, *Forothermore*, opened at the Museum of Contemporary Art Chicago in 2022 and went on to the Guggenheim.

### What he made

He is best known for the **Soundsuits**, wearable sculptures he has made since 1992. He builds them from found materials such as twigs, raffia, dyed hair, buttons, and sequins, gathered at flea markets. He made the first one from twigs in response to the police beating of Rodney King. He had picked the twigs up in a park because they looked discarded, and he built a sculpture from them before he saw that it could be worn. "The moment I put it on, I felt protected. I felt shielded from the world outside," he says in the Guggenheim's audio guide. "And yet the moment I moved in it, it made sounds." A Soundsuit covers the wearer completely, so race, gender, and class disappear inside it. He has called it a second skin and a suit of armor. The name comes from what he heard when he first put one on: the suit makes sound as the body moves. Worn by dancers, the suits rustle, whoosh, and rattle.

### Rebuilding his work

A Soundsuit is made to be worn, and it makes its sound when the body moves. The sketch keeps both: thousands of strands hang from the bones of a moving figure and hide it, and the rustle follows the speed of the limbs. With a phone on the cable, the figure is whoever stands in front of the camera.

- [**Soundsuit**](Soundsuit/): a dyed-raffia suit, after the simpler single-color Soundsuits.

  Thousands of instanced strands hang from the bones of a dancing figure and hide it completely. The suit rustles through a breath-voice `Synth`, at the speed the limbs move. It dances on its own. With a tethered iPhone in Body mode, it dresses whoever is in front of the camera instead. The suit follows the live skeleton's world anchor, joint rotations, and scale.
