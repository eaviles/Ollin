## Georg Nees

**Georg Nees** (1926-2016) was a German mathematician who worked at Siemens, where he wrote programs for a **Zuse Graphomat Z64** flatbed plotter. In February 1965 he showed the results at the Studiengalerie of the Technische Hochschule Stuttgart, which is generally counted as the first exhibition of computer-generated art anywhere. His doctoral thesis, *Generative Computergraphik* (1969), is one of the founding texts of the field, and the word "generative" in the sense used today comes from it.

**"Schotter" (1968)** is his best-known plate, and probably the most reproduced single image in generative art. Its name is German for gravel. A grid of identical squares is ruled perfectly at the top of the page, and each row down is turned and pushed a little further out of place, until the bottom of the sheet is rubble. Nothing about it is composed. One rule runs down the page, and the picture is what the rule does, which is the argument the whole field was built on.

### Recreations here

- [**Schotter**](Schotter/): a grid of squares falling out of order down the page, after Nees's *Schotter* (1968). An original Ollin interpretation written from the work and from published descriptions of how it was made, with knobs for how steeply the disorder arrives and a seed that fixes the fall.
