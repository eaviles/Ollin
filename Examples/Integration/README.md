## Integration

These sketches talk to the other apps and gear in a rig. Control comes in over OSC, MIDI, a shared network tempo session, a serial board, a Bluetooth device, a game controller, or a phone's browser. Visuals go out (and come in) over Syphon, as a system-wide virtual camera, as stage light, as LEDs, or as a laser. The Mac's own screen can be taken as material. Several machines can join into one room and draw one piece. Touch can go back under the hand as a felt pattern.

Each integration lives in its own library, so a sketch links only what it uses. The imports are `import OllinOSC`, `import OllinMIDI`, `import OllinLink`, `import OllinSerial`, `import OllinBluetooth`, `import OllinRemote`, `import OllinRoom`, `import OllinDMX`, `import OllinLaser`, `import OllinSyphon`, `import OllinCamera`, `import OllinScreen`, `import OllinController`, `import OllinHaptics`. The references are [OSC](../../Docs/Integration/OSC.md), [TUIO](../../Docs/Integration/TUIO.md), [MIDI](../../Docs/Integration/MIDI.md), [Link](../../Docs/Integration/Link.md), [Serial](../../Docs/Integration/Serial.md), [Bluetooth](../../Docs/Integration/Bluetooth.md), [Remote](../../Docs/Integration/Remote.md), [Room](../../Docs/Integration/Room.md), [DMX](../../Docs/Integration/DMX.md), [Laser](../../Docs/Integration/Laser.md), [Syphon](../../Docs/Integration/Syphon.md), [Virtual camera](../../Docs/Integration/VirtualCamera.md), [Screen capture](../../Docs/Integration/ScreenCapture.md), [Game controllers](../../Docs/Integration/Controller.md), and [Haptics](../../Docs/Integration/Haptics.md).

Several of these run both ends themselves, so they need no second app and no hardware. The round trip you watch travels over a real loopback connection.

| Example | What it shows |
|---|---|
| [OSCLoopback](OSCLoopback/Sketch.swift) | a self-contained round trip: the sketch sends an animated position to itself on `127.0.0.1` and draws the dot from what it *receives*, so the picture shows the round trip, and no second app is needed (`OSCSender`, `OSCReceiver`) |
| [OSCMonitor](OSCMonitor/Sketch.swift) | listens for OSC and prints and draws every message: point TouchOSC or any OSC source at this Mac to see what its controls send (`OSCReceiver.messages()`) |
| [TUIOSurface](TUIOSurface/Sketch.swift) | a tangible surface with nothing plugged in: a stand-in tracker sends real TUIO frames to `127.0.0.1`, and the touches, the turning marker, and the shape are all drawn from what comes back off the wire; turn `simulate` off and point a real table or phone app at port 3333 (`TUIOReceiver`, `TUIOCursor`, `TUIOObject`, `TUIOBlob`) |
| [MIDILoopback](MIDILoopback/Sketch.swift) | a self-contained loop: a virtual source sends stepped CC to itself, and a bound, smoothed `@Param` glides toward each step, so it runs with no hardware (`MIDIOutput.openVirtual`, `MIDIInput.bind`, `@Param` smoothing) |
| [MIDIMonitor](MIDIMonitor/Sketch.swift) | lists every MIDI source and prints and draws each message: connect a controller, touch each knob and pad, and see what it sends (`MIDIInput.messages()`) |
| [Timecode](Timecode/Sketch.swift) | a sketch that chases a timeline: an internal deck sends MIDI Time Code quarter frames to itself through a virtual source, a `TimecodeClock` follows them, and cues flash under a scrolling timeline; point a real sender at the Mac and the same sketch chases that (`TimecodeClock`, `Timecode`, `MIDIOutput.send(timecode:)`) |
| [Tempo](Tempo/Sketch.swift) | visuals locked to a beat from one of two clocks, and a parameter switches between them. The first is MIDI clock: an internal timer sends the sync messages to itself and a `TempoClock` follows them, and if you point a DAW or DJ mixer at the Mac, the clock follows that instead. The second is the network's shared tempo session, a `LinkClock`, so every Link-enabled app in the room lands on the same downbeat: run two copies and they pulse together, and alone it free-runs. A beat throb, bar dots, and an eight-beat orbit follow whichever clock is picked (`TempoClock`, `MIDIOutput.openVirtual`, `LinkClock`) |
| [SerialLoopback](SerialLoopback/Sketch.swift) | the physical-computing loop with no board: a fake device on one side of a pty pair prints a sensor number thirty times a second, and a real `SerialPort` reads the other side, so the trace shows what arrived over the wire. A click writes a line back and the wave flips (`SerialPort`, `latestLine`) |
| [Firmata](Firmata/Sketch.swift) | a board with no firmware of your own: StandardFirmata's pins answer to the sketch, the analog pins as bars, digital pins as dots, a click flipping the LED, and the mouse setting a PWM duty |
| [SerialMonitor](SerialMonitor/Sketch.swift) | every serial device on this Mac, live: the first USB one opens by itself, its lines scroll up the screen, a numeric line also fills a value bar, and after an unplug it reconnects on its own. The keys 1-9 open a specific device, and space writes back (`SerialPort.availableDevices()`, `matching:`) |
| [BluetoothRoom](BluetoothRoom/Sketch.swift) | every Bluetooth device around the Mac, drawn as a room: the Mac sits at the center and each device sits at the distance its signal suggests, so a phone carried past moves a dot. It needs no gear of your own, because a room is already full of devices announcing themselves (`BluetoothScan`, `BluetoothPeripheral`) |
| [BluetoothSensor](BluetoothSensor/Sketch.swift) | one Bluetooth device, connected and read: type part of a name into a parameter, and every value the device offers appears as it arrives. A heart rate drives the disc, and the battery is asked for on a timer (`BluetoothDevice`, `readings()`, `poll`) |
| [RemoteSurface](RemoteSurface/Sketch.swift) | a tunable aurora served to your phone: a `RemoteInspector` on the extension seam starts a local-network server, so every `@Param` appears as a touch control in any browser on the same Wi-Fi, and changes travel live in both directions (`RemoteInspector`) |
| [RoomCanvas](RoomCanvas/Sketch.swift) | one piece across several machines: two Macs on the same network find each other by the room's name alone, share every parameter, take one seat each so the beads travel across a wall as wide as the room, and run on one shared clock instead of each machine's own. Run alone, it is one seat of one (`Room`, `room.time`, `room.seat`) |
| [RoomLoopback](RoomLoopback/Sketch.swift) | the room with no second machine: two rooms inside one sketch trade values over a transport that never leaves the process. The left panel sends where the pointer is, the right panel draws only what arrived, and the space bar cuts the wire (`Room`, `RoomTransport`) |
| [DMXLoopback](DMXLoopback/Sketch.swift) | a full DMX round trip on screen: a `DMXSender` chases color through universe 1 over sACN on loopback, and the drawn stage of pars is lit from the *received* channels. Point the sender at a node's IP and the same universe drives real lights (`DMXSender`, `DMXReceiver`, `DMXFixture`) |
| [LEDMapping](LEDMapping/Sketch.swift) | the canvas sent out as LEDs: an `LEDMap` lays a 48-LED strip along a wave and a 12x8 matrix over the picture, samples the rendered pixels under them on the GPU each frame, and lights the drawn hardware from the universes that came back (`LEDMap`, `DMXSender`) |
| [LaserPreview](LaserPreview/Sketch.swift) | what a show laser makes of a drawing: the optimized point stream is drawn the way the beam traces it, dark travel included, with the parameters that decide what a frame costs and a readout of the point budget (`LaserFrame`, `LaserOptimizer`, `drawLaserPreview`) |
| [SyphonLoopback](SyphonLoopback/Sketch.swift) | publishes its own frames as a Syphon source *and* subscribes to them, so the recursive feedback-tunnel inset shows the round trip. Open Syphon's Simple Client to see the same frames in another app (`publishSyphon`, `SyphonClient`) |
| [SyphonViewer](SyphonViewer/Sketch.swift) | subscribes to any external Syphon source (openFrameworks, Resolume, …) and draws it letterboxed, and while it waits it lists the sources it sees (`SyphonClient`, `availableServers`) |
| [VirtualCamera](VirtualCamera/Sketch.swift) | publishes the sketch to the system-wide **Ollin Camera**, so Photo Booth, QuickTime, Zoom, and any browser read it as a live webcam. The scene is a broadcast-style "on air" picture, so the feed is obviously live. It needs the camera extension installed once, and it explains what to do when the extension is missing (`publishVirtualCamera`) |
| [ScreenCapture](ScreenCapture/Sketch.swift) | the Mac's own screen as material: a whole display, one app, or a single window arrives as a live image you can draw and filter, and the `tunnel` parameter keeps the sketch inside its own capture, so the picture recedes into itself (`ScreenCapture`) |
| [ControllerInput](ControllerInput/Sketch.swift) | a game controller as a drawing instrument: the left stick steers a pen that leaves ink, the trigger sets how heavy the line is, and the shoulder buttons turn its heading. On a pad that reports motion, tilting the pad tips the canvas, so the ink runs downhill (`Controller`, `ControllerButton`) |
| [HapticRidges](HapticRidges/Sketch.swift) | a plate of ridges you drag across and feel: three strips of different spacing and crispness knock under the pointer as it crosses them, holding the button plays a hum whose strength follows the hand, and the bottom edge draws what the hardware was actually asked for (`HapticPattern`, `playHaptic`, `TrackpadPlan`) |

Run one with `swift run Example-Integration-<Name>`, e.g. `swift run Example-Integration-OSCLoopback`.
